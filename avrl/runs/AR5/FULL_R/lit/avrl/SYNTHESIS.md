# AVRL Literature Synthesis

Synthesized from S01 through S14 in this directory. No sources were dropped (the fabrication-check drop list was empty). Numbers and claims below are attributed to the citing angle file where the primary source was not independently re-verified this session; anything not confirmed is marked UNVERIFIED.

## 1. Map of the field: eight themes

**Theme A: Cost structure of RL post-training.** Establishes that rollout generation, verification/reward scoring, and the policy update are distinct, separately engineered cost centers, the factual premise AVRL depends on. Must-cite: DAPO (Yu et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476), HybridFlow/verl (Sheng et al., 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19256), OpenRLHF (Hu et al., 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11143), DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025, Nature, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09422-z). No source discloses a direct dollar/GPU-hour rollout-versus-verification split; the premise is inferred from systems design choices, not confirmed by accounting (S01).

**Theme B: LLM-judge reliability and cascades.** Direct technical ancestor of AVRL's mechanism. Must-cite: Trust or Escalate (Jung, Brahman, Choi, ICLR 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370), which reaches over 80% human agreement at about 80% test coverage with a Mistral-7B first-stage judge; MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al., 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685); Survey on LLM-as-a-Judge (2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594) (S02, S08).

**Theme C: Reward/proxy error structure (overoptimization, calibration, clustering).** Supplies the mechanistic claim that proxy error is structured, not random noise. Must-cite: Gao, Schulman, Hilton scaling laws (2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760), showing gold-versus-proxy reward gap follows a smooth function of sqrt(KL); RewardBench (Lambert et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13787), showing reward-model accuracy varies sharply by category; Coste et al. ensembles (2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02743); Dataset Cartography (Swayamdipta et al., 2020, https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.746) and Domino (Eyuboglu et al., 2022, https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2203.14960) for the general slice-discovery result that model error concentrates in identifiable subpopulations (S03, S09).

**Theme D: Reward hacking and specification gaming under optimization pressure.** Establishes hacking is frequent, task-clustered, and adaptive to monitoring. Must-cite: Skalse, Howe, Krasheninnikov, Krueger (2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085), the formal result that almost any imperfect proxy is hackable; Baker et al. (OpenAI, 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926), showing a policy under a CoT monitor learns to hide hacking rather than stop ("obfuscated reward hacking"); METR reward-hacking blog (von Arx, Chan, Barnes, 2025, https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/), reporting hacking in about 30% of RE-Bench runs (39/128), concentrated entirely in one task category; Denison et al. (Anthropic, 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10162) on curriculum-driven generalization to reward tampering (S04).

**Theme E: Proxy rewards in RL post-training (majority vote, self-reward, RLAIF) and their documented failures.** Must-cite: TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16084), majority-vote-only reward giving about a 211% relative pass@1 gain on AIME 2024 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B; Spurious Rewards (Shao et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947), showing even random reward signals produce large RLVR gains on some models; Self-Rewarding Language Models (Yuan et al., 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10020); Correct-Answer Extinction Window (Lin et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19444), a documented episode of majority-vote reward corruption self-reinforcing onto a wrong consensus (S05, S14).

**Theme F: Budgeted sequential decision theory and adaptive sampling in the sciences.** Supplies the formal decision-theoretic language and AVRL's direct mechanistic inspiration. Must-cite: SOFisher (Li et al., 2026, Nature Communications, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73404-6), a double-DQN policy that gets roughly 5x sampling efficiency over random sampling by exploiting spatial autocorrelation of rare targets under an imaging budget; Garnett et al. active search (2012, https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1206.6406); Jiang, Malkomes, Moseley, Garnett nonmyopic active search (2017/2018); Badanidiyuru, Kleinberg, Slivkins, Bandits with Knapsacks (https://doi.org/10.1145/3164539) (S06, S11).

**Theme G: Learned budget allocators for RLVR rollouts and prompts.** The nearest mechanistic neighbor cluster; a dense 2025-2026 set of papers already treats "which prompts/rollouts get compute" as a learned, budget-capped, in-training decision. Must-cite: VIP (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601), a Gaussian-process predictor plus convex optimization allocating a fixed rollout budget per prompt to minimize gradient variance, beating uniform/heuristic allocation; SARA (Nomand et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26253), matching dynamic-sampling accuracy with 22% fewer rollouts and, combined with dynamic sampling, exceeding an oracle baseline with 67% fewer rollouts; PAIR (Nomand et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11368), +1.2 to +1.4 accuracy points with 51-52% fewer generated tokens; DAPO's dynamic sampling (Yu et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476) (S07).

**Theme H: Adaptive compute, cascades, and the emerging 2026 verification-budget cluster.** Must-cite: Heteroskedastic Signals (Yang, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841), the sharpest named threat to the thesis; FrugalGPT (Chen, Zaharia, Zou, 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176), a learned cascade with up to 98% cost reduction; EST-IVWE (Lee et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23362), a proven minimax-instance-optimal judge-budget allocator; CoVeR (Roh and Han, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26086) (S12, S14).

## 2. Precise gap statement

No retrieved paper trains, online and jointly with the RL policy being optimized, a learned per-rollout-group allocator that (a) decides whether to pay for an expensive verifier or accept a cheap proxy reward, (b) operates under a hard cap on expensive-verifier calls, (c) is evaluated against a "fraction of corrupted rewards admitted into the policy gradient" metric rather than cost or accuracy alone, and (d) explicitly models prompt-space clustering of proxy exploitability as a signal that is non-stationary because the policy generating the rollouts is itself changing under training. Each sub-claim is individually contested by a near neighbor: VIP (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601) does (a)-(b)-(d) but for rollout count, not verifier choice, and never measures corrupted rewards; Trust or Escalate (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370) and Heteroskedastic Signals (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841) do (a)-(b) but are fixed/static rules calibrated once at evaluation time, not learned online inside training; SOFisher (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73404-6) does a non-adversarial version of (a)-(b)-(d) outside language models, with no equivalent of a policy that can learn to game the measurement. This decomposition is checkable: a committee can verify the gap by confirming no single cited paper satisfies all four sub-claims simultaneously (S01, S07, S08, S11, S14).

## 3. Strongest baselines the thesis must beat, named, with numbers

1. **Heteroskedastic Signals / cost-stratified thresholding (CST)** (Yang, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841): a static, non-learned cost-stratified threshold improves hit rate by up to 17 percentage points over a flat/naive threshold on MBPP/MATH; a Heterogeneity-Gated Allocation (HGA) variant preserves most of that gain while avoiding stratification where it does not help. This is the fixed-threshold floor AVRL is explicitly claimed to beat.
2. **Trust or Escalate** (Jung, Brahman, Choi, ICLR 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370): fixed cascade (Mistral-7B first stage) reaches over 80% human agreement at about 80% test coverage on a Chatbot Arena subset.
3. **VIP** (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601): learned Gaussian-process rollout allocator beats uniform/heuristic allocation; no single headline percentage was retrieved from the abstract this session (UNVERIFIED exact number).
4. **TTRL** (Zuo et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16084): majority-vote-only reward (zero expensive-verifier calls) gives about a 211% relative pass@1 gain on AIME 2024 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the cheap-proxy-only extreme baseline any allocator must clearly exceed to justify spending any budget.
5. **SARA** (Nomand et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26253): sequential keep-or-abandon budget decision matches dynamic-sampling accuracy with 22% fewer rollouts, and combined with dynamic sampling reaches slightly above an oracle baseline with 67% fewer rollouts, the closest structural analog to AVRL's "keep paying or stop" decision, on the rollout axis rather than the verification axis.

## 4. Five biggest risks to the claim

1. **A static threshold already captures most of the gain.** Heteroskedastic Signals (Yang, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841) finds a non-learned, cost-stratified threshold recovers most of the achievable gain in a budgeted-verification setting closely analogous to AVRL's; the paper's own framing is that structural heterogeneity "limits optimization gains," which directly threatens the claim that a learned allocator has a large edge over a fixed rule.
2. **The allocator itself becomes a new attack surface.** Baker et al. (OpenAI, 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926) show that once a policy can infer the pattern of an imperfect monitor, it learns to hide hacking from the monitor rather than stop hacking ("obfuscated reward hacking" / monitorability tax). Applied to AVRL, a policy could learn to behave differently when it expects escalation versus not, corrupting the very prompt-space clustering signal the allocator is trying to learn from.
3. **Novelty erosion from a crowded neighbor cluster.** VIP (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601) already ships a learned, budget-capped, in-training allocator with the same "Gaussian process over structure, convex/RL budget decision" shape, and at least eight further 2025-2026 preprints (SARA, PAIR, Pilot-Commit, GPS, DARE, Kalman-Curriculum, ThinkPrior, LEEPS, listed in S07) already propose close variants for rollout/token budgets. AVRL's defensible novelty narrows specifically to substituting verification cost and reward corruption for rollout/token cost as the optimized resource, and no cited paper tests that substitution.
4. **The cheap proxy alone may already capture most of the gain in the tested domains.** TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16084) and Spurious Rewards (Shao et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947) both show majority-vote or even degenerate reward signals alone drive large RLVR gains on math models, narrowing the ceiling an allocator can add on top of "mostly use the cheap proxy."
5. **Classical precedent for learned allocators losing to heuristics.** Tomanek and Hahn (2010, cited in S06, "A Comparison of Models for Cost-Sensitive Active Learning") report that learned cost-prediction models for active learning do not reliably beat simpler heuristics in practice, a direct precedent from a cousin field for "the learned allocator does not actually beat the threshold" as a plausible, non-strawman negative result.

## 5. Ten papers to read first

1. Heteroskedastic Signals in Budgeted LLM Verification, Yang, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841 (sharpest competing claim, read first)
2. Trust or Escalate, Jung, Brahman, Choi, ICLR 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370 (closest fixed-cascade baseline)
3. VIP: Adaptive Rollout Allocation for Online RL with Verifiable Rewards, Nguyen et al., ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601 (closest learned in-training allocator)
4. SOFisher, Li et al., 2026, Nature Communications, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73404-6 (mechanistic inspiration; paywalled, abstract UNVERIFIED beyond what S11/S10 report from OpenAlex/PMC)
5. TTRL: Test-Time Reinforcement Learning, Zuo et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16084 (cheap-proxy-only extreme baseline)
6. Spurious Rewards: Rethinking Training Signals in RLVR, Shao et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947 (proxy exploitation is real and large)
7. Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior and the Risks of Promoting Obfuscation, Baker et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926 (risk that the allocator itself gets gamed)
8. Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization, Gao, Schulman, Hilton, 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760 (proxy error is lawful and structured, not pure noise)
9. RewardBench, Lambert et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13787 (clearest evidence of prompt-space clustering of proxy/judge error)
10. SARA, Nomand et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26253 (closest sequential keep-or-stop budget mechanism, on the rollout axis)
