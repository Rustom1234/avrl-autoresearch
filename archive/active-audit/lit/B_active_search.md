# Topic B: Budgeted Active Search and Rare-Class Discovery

## SOFisher (Ng et al., Nature Communications 2026 / bioRxiv 2024)

Problem: choosing which field-of-view (FOV) to image next in spatial omics experiments, under a limited imaging/sequencing budget, so as to capture as many FOVs containing rare target tissue landmarks (TTLs, e.g. amyloid-beta plaques, neurofibrillary tangles) as possible, without needing a co-registered H&E stain to guide selection. Source: [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73404-6), [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.05.602236v1), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42185268/).

State, action, reward (as summarized from the PMC full text, [PMC13388978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13388978/)): state = current FOV position, cell-type counts within the FOV, and a 17-dimensional adaptive partition vector encoding search history over the slide. Action = discrete move to one of 24 neighboring positions (1 or 2 FOV-widths, any direction). Reward = `r(s,a) = n_k + w*r_k`, where n_k is the count of TTLs found in the current FOV and w*r_k penalizes revisiting already-sampled grid cells. Algorithm: Deep Q-Network, per the GitHub repo folder structure (`model/DQN`, `src/dqn.py`, `src/replay_dqn.py`), [github.com/Heathcliff-Ng/SOFisher](https://github.com/Heathcliff-Ng/SOFisher). UNVERIFIED: the repo README itself does not state the RL algorithm explicitly; this is inferred from file/folder names.

Baseline: random sampling (RS) is the primary comparator across experiments; the PMC summary found no mention of greedy or uncertainty-sampling baselines. Reported gain: roughly 5x improvement in sampling efficiency to capture TTL-containing FOVs versus random sampling, and consistently fewer steps needed to reach a fixed hit count (e.g., 10 TTL-containing FOVs) across 50+ steps, per [PMC13388978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13388978/). Exact numeric tables were not independently verified from primary figures; treat the "5x" figure as UNVERIFIED pending direct read of the paper's results section.

Follow-up/citing work: a related but methodologically distinct paper, S2-omics ("Smart spatial omics"), also targets region-of-interest selection for spatial omics, published in Nature Cell Biology, [nature.com/articles/s41556-025-01811-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41556-025-01811-w), and a preprint update, [bioRxiv 2025.09.21.677634](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.21.677634v1.full.pdf). Whether S2-omics is a direct citation of SOFisher or a parallel effort is UNVERIFIED from search snippets alone. No evidence was found of SOFisher, or a direct derivative of it, being applied outside tissue imaging (e.g., to text/document corpora); the paper itself only speculates, as a theoretical future direction, about extending to clinical biopsy site selection, requiring 3D navigation and safety constraints not yet implemented ([PMC13388978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13388978/)).

## Classical active search line

Garnett, Krishnamurthy et al., "Bayesian Optimal Active Search and Surveying" (ICML 2012), frames active search as Bayesian decision theory: find as many members of a rare class as possible under a query budget, deriving the optimal (myopic and lookahead) Bayesian policy rather than optimizing generalization error. [arXiv:1206.6406](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6406), [ICML PDF](https://icml.cc/2012/papers/618.pdf).

Jiang, Malkomes, Converse, Shofner, Moseley, Garnett, "Efficient Nonmyopic Active Search" (ICML 2017), proves no polynomial-time policy achieves a constant-factor approximation of the optimal expected utility, and proposes a nonmyopic policy that considers the full remaining budget and dynamically trades off exploration/exploitation without a tunable parameter; beats myopic approximations on drug discovery, materials, and citation-network data. [proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jiang17d.html](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jiang17d.html), code at [github.com/shalijiang/efficient_nonmyopic_active_search](https://github.com/shalijiang/efficient_nonmyopic_active_search). A follow-up extends this to drug and materials discovery specifically: [arXiv:1811.08871](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08871).

Diversity-aware extension: Nguyen et al., "Nonmyopic Multiclass Active Search with Diminishing Returns for Diverse Discovery" (AISTATS 2023), adds a submodular diminishing-returns utility for multi-class/diverse discovery, applied to drug discovery. [arXiv:2202.03593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03593), [MLR PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/nguyen23d/nguyen23d.pdf).

Active search with bandits: Ma, Garnett, Schneider, "Active Search on Graphs" (KDD 2013), uses sigma-optimality for graph-structured active search. [dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2487575.2487605](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2487575.2487605). Renders (Naver Labs Europe), "Active Search for High Recall: a Non-Stationary Extension of Thompson Sampling" (ECIR 2018), treats active search as a non-stationary multi-armed bandit over soft clusters, evaluated on fraud detection, e-discovery, and patent prior-art search; two-level policy selects a cluster via Thompson sampling then the most relevant item within it. [arXiv:1712.09550](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09550). Note: an earlier search query assumed a "Vanchinathan" authorship for this line; that attribution is UNVERIFIED and the paper found is credited to Renders.

Rare category detection: He and Carbonell, "Nearest-Neighbor-Based Active Learning for Rare Category Detection" (NeurIPS 2007), uses local density differentials for de-novo minority-class discovery with no initial labels. [mlanthology.org entry](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2007/he2007neurips-nearestneighborbased/). A related survey-style result, "Active Learning for Anomaly and Rare-Category Detection," is indexed at [dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/2976040.2976175](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/2976040.2976175); exact authorship/year was not independently confirmed from the snippet, UNVERIFIED.

Active anomaly detection under budget: Li et al., "Deep Anomaly Detection under Labeling Budget Constraints" (ICML 2023), derives theoretical conditions for anomaly-score generalization from labeled queries and proposes budget-optimal coverage strategies. [arXiv:2302.07832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07832).

Adjacent to the corpus-audit setting: "Similarity Search for Efficient Active Learning and Search of Rare Concepts" studies using embedding-similarity search to accelerate rare-concept discovery at scale. [arXiv:2007.00077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00077). This is the closest classical-line paper conceptually to "search a large corpus for rare targets using similarity," though it is not framed as document/dataset auditing.

## Budgeted auditing of datasets with expensive labels

Benchmark contamination search: a 2023 survey/study on data contamination in LLM benchmarks catalogs five detection families (string-matching, likelihood-based, membership inference, LLM-prompted detection, benchmark auditing). [arXiv:2311.09783](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09783v2). A 2026 paper, "The Reliability Gap in Benchmark Auditing," shows no contamination-detection method is reliable across contamination tiers and access settings. [arXiv:2606.03305](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03305). These are detection-method papers, not sequential/budgeted document-selection policies; none frame the "which document to check next" step as active search with a state/action/reward or a nonmyopic policy.

Copyright and memorization auditing: "SoK: Dataset Copyright Auditing in Machine Learning Systems" surveys the field. [arXiv:2410.16618](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16618). "On the Evidentiary Limits of Membership Inference for Copyright Auditing" and "RECAP" address extraction/verification methods, not query-budget allocation across documents. [arXiv:2601.12937](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12937), [arXiv:2510.25941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25941).

PII auditing under budget: "SnapAudit," an active-auditing framework for differentially-private in-context learning, is the closest budget-aware, adaptive auditing method found, but it targets DP noise/privacy-budget estimation via simulated snapshots, not selecting which real records or documents to review. [arXiv:2511.13502](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13502). General PII-audit guidance found was process/compliance oriented, not an adaptive statistical method.

## Answers

1. Structural property needed to beat random and static ranking: the positive/rare class must be locally correlated in some queryable feature or relational space, so that a label observed at one point is informative about nearby unlabeled points. In SOFisher this is spatial autocorrelation of tissue landmarks across neighboring FOVs (state includes spatial history, PMC13388978 above). In the Garnett/Jiang line it is smoothness under a kernel (Gaussian process) or graph structure (Ma/Garnett/Schneider). In rare-category detection it is local density. Without such structure, no adaptive policy can beat a well-calibrated static ranking, since there is nothing to propagate from queried to unqueried items.

2. Off-the-shelf baseline a new active-search-over-corpus paper must beat: (a) random sampling, the near-universal baseline in every paper reviewed above (SOFisher, Jiang et al., Renders, Li et al.), and (b) a static one-shot ranking by a cheap proxy score (embedding similarity to known positives, perplexity, or an LLM-judge score run once and ranked), since Jiang et al. explicitly show myopic/one-shot approximations are beaten by nonmyopic budget-aware policies, implying static ranking is the real competitive bar, not just random. [proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jiang17d.html](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jiang17d.html).

3. Has anyone already framed "which documents to audit next" as active search: no paper meeting that exact description was found in this search. Benchmark-contamination, copyright/memorization, and PII-auditing literature address detection methods or one-shot ranking, and SnapAudit is adaptive but not document-selection. This is a negative result from a search pass, not a proof of absence: mark UNVERIFIED / not confirmed to exist, rather than confirmed absent.
