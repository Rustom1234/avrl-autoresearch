## Captions

**Figure 1.** Relative gain of the self-trained online allocator (`greedy_online`) over a budget-matched fixed threshold (`static_matched`) at 25% verification budget, plotted against each point's cheap-feature AUC for proxy error. Error bars are 95% bootstrap CIs (300 group resamples x 8 policy seeds); point area scales linearly with the point's proxy error rate; the dashed line marks zero gain. Blue points are exact-answer tasks (GSM8K, MBPP); orange points are the judge-scored task (HelpSteer2). E09c is drawn as a hollow marker because its CI includes zero (inconclusive).

**Figure 1b.** The same six points and the same y-axis, with proxy error rate in place of cheap-feature AUC on the x-axis. Same encodings: error bars are 95% bootstrap CIs, point area scales with proxy error rate, blue/orange marks task type, E09c is hollow (inconclusive).

## What the figure shows, and what it does not

The figure shows that `greedy_online` beats the budget-matched threshold (`static_matched`) at 25% budget almost everywhere tested: five of the six plotted points have a 95% CI that excludes zero on the positive side, with point estimates from +8.3% (E08) to +25.7% (E09b), and the same holds project-wide on every bootstrapped comparison except one (six of the seven points with a `bootstrap.json`, including the original E09; E09c is the sole inconclusive exception). It does not show the `ts_online` cell-bandit allocator at all, a separate policy tested in the same ledger entries (M01b, L9a-d, L10a) and left off this plot for clarity, not because it under- or out-performs `greedy_online`. It also no longer shows a clean AUC-predicts-gain relationship: E01 (the strongest cheap-feature AUC plotted, 0.923) gains +19.2%, comparable to the weak-AUC judge-scored points, so cheap AUC alone does not separate the points with a significant gain from the one without one (E09c, hollow, CI [-5.7%, +33.7%]).
