## Caption

**Figure 3 (`fig3_code`).** Held-out MBPP pass@1 (200 problems, full assert set) over training, one panel per code experiment. Panel A, E10: OLMo 2 1B base with a signature-vote cheap grader; full and proxy (grey, solid/dashed) are the reference arms, threshold (blue) and learned (orange) are the budgeted allocators. Panel B, E10b: OLMo 2 1B Instruct with a visible-test cheap grader and a 108-problem curriculum; only the proxy arm (grey dashed) has a complete run so far. Dashed grey horizontal line: untrained Instruct reference. Dotted grey horizontal line: untrained base reference. Final HumanEval pass@1 is printed in each arm's legend label when the run's final step logged it.

## What the figure shows

E10's proxy arm, which buys zero expensive labels under a cheap grader that is wrong on about 99 percent of groups, collapses below both the threshold and learned arms and ends near the untrained base's MBPP floor. E10b's proxy arm, under the same zero-expensive-call budget but a visible-test cheap grader on an easier 108-problem curriculum, instead climbs past the untrained Instruct reference (0.220 vs 0.145 final MBPP) with no expensive calls at all, so cheap-grader reliability, not the allocator budget, is what separates the two outcomes.

## Caveat (added 2026-10-02 20:50 UTC)
The MBPP curves use a 200-problem subset drawn with the run seed; the dashed reference lines were evaluated on the seed-0 subset (local evaluations for E10b's panel), so seed 1 and 2 curves are comparable to each other only through HumanEval (fixed 164 problems, legend) and to the reference only approximately on MBPP. Corruption counts in Figure 2 are unaffected.
