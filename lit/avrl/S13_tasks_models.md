# S13: Tasks and Models for AVRL Experiments

## Contribution to the thesis (3 sentences)

This angle supplies the concrete testbed where a learned verification allocator can show its edge over a fixed threshold: math and code domains where the expensive verifier (execution sandbox, AIME grading, LLM judge) has a real, documented cost and a known failure mode (reward hacking, majority-vote collapse on hard items), unlike settings where cost is simulated. It fixes the compute envelope too: small open models (Qwen2.5/Qwen3 1.5B to 7B, Llama 3.2) on 4x A100 40GB is the regime used by recent RLVR replications (DAPO, SimpleRL-Zoo, Open-Reasoner-Zero, TTRL), so baselines and rollout costs from those papers are reusable for AVRL's budget accounting instead of invented. Finally, this is where the "prompt-space structure" claim is testable at all: math and code datasets have known difficulty strata and known verifier-failure clusters, the same kind of exploitable autocorrelation SOFisher used in imaging space.

## Sources (16)

1. Cobbe et al., 2021, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168. GSM8K, 8.5K grade-school math problems, introduced a trained verifier as the scoring step, the original "verification is costly and separate" case.
2. Hendrycks et al., 2021, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03874. MATH dataset, 12,500 competition problems across 5 difficulty levels, labels give ready-made prompt-space structure to test difficulty-based routing.
3. Jain et al., 2024, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974. LiveCodeBench, contamination-free coding benchmark scored by held-out test execution, a clean expensive-verifier baseline close to ground truth.
4. Liu et al., 2023, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01210. EvalPlus/HumanEval+, found HumanEval's original tests under-test correctness, so models scoring high there score lower under expanded tests, direct evidence a cheap verifier is exploitable.
5. Jimenez et al., 2023, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770. SWE-bench, 2,294 GitHub issue/PR pairs, original paper found Claude 2 resolved 1.96% of issues, an expensive (full repo test suite) and very sparse reward setting.
6. Yu et al., 2025, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476. DAPO, open-source RL system, 50 points on AIME 2024 from Qwen2.5-32B base, first fully open code/data/hyperparameters RLVR recipe at this scale.
7. Hu et al., 2025, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24290. Open-Reasoner-Zero, reports about 1/10 the training steps of DeepSeek-R1-Zero to reach comparable AIME2024/MATH500/GPQA-Diamond scores on the same Qwen2.5-32B base.
8. Zeng et al., 2025, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18892. SimpleRL-Zoo, systematic zero-RL study across open base models including small (1.5B-7B) sizes, documents where small-model zero-RL succeeds or collapses.
9. DeepSeek-AI, 2025, Nature, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09422-z. DeepSeek-R1, the RLVR recipe the small-scale replications above reproduce, sets the large-scale reference point AVRL's budget-capped runs compare against.
10. Zuo et al., 2025, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16084. TTRL, majority vote as reward with no labels, reports about 211% relative gain in Qwen2.5-Math-7B pass@1 on AIME 2024 from proxy-only signal.
11. Qu et al., 2025, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04632. MoPPS, models per-prompt success rate as a latent variable with Bayesian/bandit posterior sampling, cuts rollout count while preserving RL quality, no extra LLM calls needed.
12. Yang et al., 2024, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15115. Qwen2.5 technical report, base model family (0.5B-72B) used by nearly every RLVR replication above, the standard small-model anchor.
13. Qwen Team, 2025, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388. Qwen3 technical report, hybrid thinking/non-thinking modes, gives a built-in cost axis since longer thinking-mode rollouts cost more to verify.
14. Jung, Brahman, and Choi, 2024 (ICLR 2025 per thesis framing), arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370. Trust or Escalate, confidence-based cascade escalating cheap to strong judge, over 80% human agreement at about 80% test coverage on Chatbot Arena with Mistral-7B. The fixed-cascade baseline AVRL must beat.
15. Nguyen et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601. VIP, allocates a fixed rollout budget per prompt to minimize expected policy-gradient variance via Gaussian-process prediction, reports improved sample efficiency over uniform allocation.
16. Yang, 2026, arXiv, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841. Heteroskedastic Signals in Budgeted LLM Verification, shows uncertainty-signal quality varies by cost stratum, a static cost-stratified threshold captures up to 17 points of the available gain, adaptive optimization over a shared signal adds little more.

## What supports the claim, what threatens it

Supports: EvalPlus (4) is direct evidence cheap verifiers are exploitable in exactly the proposed code domains, giving AVRL a concrete reward-hacking target. TTRL (10) shows proxy-only reward (majority vote) is strong on math with a small open model, so "accept the cheap proxy sometimes" is a viable action, not a degenerate one. MoPPS (11) shows a learned, Bayesian, per-prompt online predictor beats static selection on the closely related axis of which prompts to roll out, some transfer to "which rollouts to verify expensively" is plausible. SWE-bench and LiveCodeBench (3, 5) give genuinely expensive, slow verifiers where a budget constraint is real, not simulated.

Threatens: Heteroskedastic Signals (16) is the biggest risk. In the same budgeted-LLM-verification framing as this thesis, it argues most of the achievable gain over a naive baseline comes from correct static stratification, not a learned adaptive policy, and that further optimization over a shared signal adds little. If that generalizes from evaluation-time selective verification to in-training RL allocation, it directly undercuts the "learned beats fixed threshold" claim. VIP (15) already claims a learned, variance-aware, per-prompt rollout-budget allocator with gains over uniform allocation in RLVR, so AVRL's contribution needs to be sharply distinguished (verification cost vs. rollout count) or it reads as incremental. TTRL's large single-proxy win (10) cuts the other way too: if majority vote alone recovers most of the gain on math tasks, the ceiling for an allocator to add value on top of "always use the cheap proxy" may be narrow in exactly these domains.

## Closest prior work from this angle

VIP (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv 2602.01601, source 15) is the closest: a learned, per-prompt, budget-capped allocator operating during RLVR training on the same math/code task families proposed here. It differs in what is allocated: VIP decides how many rollouts to spend per prompt to minimize policy-gradient variance, holding the verification method fixed, while AVRL proposes deciding, per rollout group, whether to pay for the expensive verifier at all versus accepting a cheap proxy, treating verification cost and corrupted-reward risk (not gradient variance) as the managed resource, and explicitly tracking corrupted-reward admission as a metric VIP does not report. A second distinction is exploitation dynamics: VIP's Gaussian-process model is a within-run efficiency tool, while AVRL's proposal requires learning where the cheap proxy is systematically wrong as the policy improves (a non-stationary, adversarial signal), closer in spirit to SOFisher's exploitation of spatial autocorrelation than to VIP's variance-minimization objective.
