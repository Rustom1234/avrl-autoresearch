# S11: SOFisher and Adaptive Sampling in the Sciences (framing angle for AVRL related work)

## 1. What this angle contributes (three sentences)

This angle shows that casting "what to measure next under a budget" as a sequential decision problem already has a working track record outside language models, in spatial omics, materials discovery, and drug screening, so AVRL is extending a proven paradigm rather than inventing the framing from scratch. SOFisher is the direct mechanistic precedent: a learned policy (double DQN) chooses where to spend an expensive-measurement budget next and exploits local structure (spatial autocorrelation of rare targets) to beat fixed allocation by a wide margin, the closest existing demonstration that "a learned allocator beats a fixed rule at equal budget by exploiting structure in the thing being sampled." The gap this angle also exposes is that none of these science systems has an adversarial or gameable proxy signal, so this literature supports the allocator-beats-fixed-threshold half of the AVRL claim but offers no precedent for the reward-hacking-resistance half.

## 3. Support versus threat to the AVRL thesis claim

Supports the claim: SOFisher is direct existence proof that a learned, budget-capped allocator beats a fixed non-learned rule at matched budget by exploiting structure in what is being sampled (approximately 5x efficiency over random sampling, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73404-6), the same shape as "learned allocator beats fixed threshold at equal expensive-call budget." S2-omics shows a cheap proxy can stand in for an expensive ground-truth assay and still recover most of what expensive, expert-driven selection would find (89.3% coverage, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-025-01811-w), supporting the premise that cheap proxies are usable substitutes most of the time. The restless-bandit framing of self-driving microscopy (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14930) and the Bayesian active-learning drug-screening platform (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55287-7) both show the field already treats "what to measure next under budget" as a first-class sequential decision problem, giving methodological precedent for treating verifier purchase as an RL action.

Threatens the claim: none of these systems has an adversarial or policy-conditional proxy signal. A tissue field of view does or does not contain the target regardless of what the imaging policy has learned; spatial autocorrelation is a benign, stable structure to exploit, not a weakness a co-evolving policy can learn to game. AVRL's setting differs in a way this angle cannot speak to: its proxy is scored against a model that is itself changing during training, so proxy errors can become correlated with what the trained policy is learning to do, a reward-hacking dynamic this literature has no analog for. Every comparison here is learned-policy-versus-heuristic (random, grid, manual pathologist) at a single budget, not learned-policy-versus-calibrated-fixed-threshold, so this angle supports the general paradigm but not the harder comparator the thesis needs to beat; that comparator instead comes from the ML-side literature (the heteroskedastic budgeted verification line, arXiv 2606.15841, outside this angle's search and not independently checked here).

## 4. Closest prior work from this angle, and how it differs

The closest prior work from this angle is SOFisher itself (Li et al., 2026, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73404-6). It differs from the AVRL proposal in three ways. First, SOFisher's measurement outcome is fixed and truthful: a field of view genuinely contains or does not contain the target, so there is no equivalent of a gameable cheap proxy, only a question of where to look. Second, SOFisher's policy and its environment are decoupled: the tissue slide does not change in response to what the imaging policy has learned, whereas in AVRL the object being measured is the policy under training, which co-adapts with the allocator's choices across the run, a feedback loop absent from SOFisher. Third, SOFisher operates within a single episode (one slide, one fixed step budget) rather than across a training run that must also track where a cheap proxy becomes unreliable as the trained policy shifts; SOFisher never has to detect drift in its own measurement's validity, only where to look next for a stable ground truth.
