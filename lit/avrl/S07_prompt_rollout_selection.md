# S07: Prompt and Rollout Selection in RLVR (2025-2026)

## 1. What this angle contributes to the thesis

This literature establishes that RLVR compute is dominated by rollout generation and is spent very unevenly across prompts, since many prompt groups carry near-zero learning signal (all-correct or all-wrong), which is the structural premise AVRL's allocator exploits. A dense 2026 cluster (VIP, SARA, PAIR, Pilot-Commit, GPS, DARE, Kalman-Curriculum, ThinkPrior, LEEPS) already treats "which prompts or rollouts get compute" as a learned, budget-capped, in-training decision, giving AVRL both a design template (predictive models fit on reward/success history) and a set of direct competitors. Critically, none of these optimize against verification cost as the scarce resource; they assume the reward signal is free or instant once a rollout is generated, so they demonstrate the allocator idea works for rollout-generation budgets but not that it transfers when the expensive, corruptible resource is the judge call itself.

## 3. Support vs. threat to the thesis claim

**Supports:** The cluster shows large, real efficiency gains from moving off uniform/fixed allocation toward a fitted, per-prompt or per-group model (Pilot-Commit 4.0x, SARA 67% fewer rollouts, PAIR 51-52% fewer tokens), which is direct precedent for "a learned allocator beats a fixed rule at equal budget" — just on the rollout-count axis rather than the verification-cost axis. Several designs (VIP's Gaussian process over per-prompt success, GPS's history-anchored difficulty model, DARE's co-evolved difficulty estimator) already lean on prompt-space structure as the exploitable signal, supporting AVRL's claim that proxy-verifier error clusters in prompt space and are learnable. SARA's sequential "decide early whether to keep paying" logic is close to a DQN-style budget policy in spirit, echoing the SOFisher inspiration, which suggests the mechanism transfers across domains.

**Threatens:** Every one of these papers optimizes rollouts-to-target-accuracy or token count, not corrupted-reward rate; none report a "fewer corrupted rewards" metric, so AVRL cannot borrow their numbers for its central claim and must build that metric and its baselines from nothing. The mechanism "learned, budget-capped, in-training allocator that exploits prompt-space structure" is no longer novel by itself — at least nine 2026 preprints in this note already propose close variants of it for rollout budgets — so AVRL's defensible novelty narrows to substituting verification cost/corruption for rollout/token cost as the optimized resource, and that substitution is the part that is currently unverified anywhere in this set. Finally, DAPO's and AdaRFT's results (large gains from a simple threshold or difficulty curriculum) echo the Heteroskedastic Signals finding already in scope for this thesis: if a cheap heuristic already captures most of the achievable gain, a learned allocator's marginal edge specifically for verification may be small.

## 4. Closest prior work from this angle

SARA ("Early Verdicts, Better Budgets," arXiv 2607.26253) is the closest match from this specific angle (excluding VIP, Trust-or-Escalate, and Heteroskedastic Signals, which the task brief already names as the overall closest work). SARA uses a Beta-posterior sequential decision rule to decide, mid-group, whether to keep committing rollout budget to a prompt or abandon it early — structurally the same "keep paying or stop" decision AVRL wants to make about the expensive verifier. It differs from AVRL in three ways: (a) the scarce resource it allocates is rollout generation, not verifier calls, so it implicitly assumes scoring is free once a rollout exists; (b) its policy is a fitted Beta-posterior/statistical rule, not a trained RL allocator, so it does not learn during training where a cheap proxy is being exploited; and (c) it has no notion of reward corruption or adversarial exploitation of a cheap signal — its objective is speed to a fixed accuracy target, not the integrity of the reward used for policy updates.
