# S05: RL with Proxy, Noisy, Partial, or Self-Generated Rewards

## 1. Contribution to the AVRL thesis (3 sentences)

This angle supplies the empirical base rate for how often cheap reward proxies (majority vote, self-consistency, self-judged or even random signals) already substitute for expensive verifiers in RL post-training, and shows that substitution is common and exploitable, the exact failure mode AVRL's learned allocator must price and control. It also supplies the closest non-allocator baselines: static or per-example confidence thresholds that AVRL must beat with a learned, budget-capped policy rather than a fixed rule. Finally it supplies the mechanism AVRL should exploit: prompt-space structure in where proxies fail, since proxy-reward reliability is not uniform across prompts, it clusters by problem type and difficulty.

## 3. Supports vs. threatens the thesis claim

Supports: Spurious Rewards (source 2) and TTRL (source 1) together show proxy signals, even degenerate or random ones, can drive large measurable training gains, confirming the reward-hacking surface is real and large, which motivates an allocator that actively monitors for it during training rather than assuming a fixed proxy stays safe. DAPO's dynamic sampling (source 6) is existence proof that prompt-space structure, per-prompt rollout-outcome variance, is already informative enough to drive a budget decision, supporting the claim that prompt-space structure is exploitable; DAPO just uses a fixed static rule rather than a learned one. Scaling Laws for RM Overoptimization (source 7) shows proxy exploitation is a lawful, structured function of optimization pressure rather than unstructured noise, supporting the claim that a learned allocator can anticipate and price it rather than only react to it.

Threatens: Heteroskedastic Signals (source 4) is the most direct threat. It finds a static, cost-stratified threshold captures most of the achievable gain, and a smarter gated rule only wins by up to 17pp when signal heterogeneity is unusually large; this undercuts the claim that a learned, in-training allocator will clearly beat a fixed threshold, at least in the evaluation-time setting it studies. AVRL needs to show the in-training, budget-capped setting differs enough, because proxy exploitability is non-stationary as the policy improves, for learning to still pay off. Self-Rewarding LMs and Constitutional AI (sources 9, 10) show that in some regimes a fully self-generated, zero-cost proxy reward works well for many iterations without any allocator, which weakens the "verifier cost is dominant and must be actively rationed" framing for those regimes. More broadly, sources 1, 2, and 9 together suggest cheap proxies already deliver most of the gain, so a learned allocator's marginal edge over "mostly use the cheap proxy, occasionally spot-check" could be small unless expensive-verifier calls are made scarce enough that allocation quality actually binds.

## 4. Closest prior work from this angle, and how AVRL differs

The single closest prior work from this angle is Trust or Escalate (Jung, Brahman, Choi, ICLR 2025, arXiv 2407.18370): a selective-evaluation judge cascade that escalates from a cheap judge to an expensive one when the cheap judge's self-reported confidence is low, with a provable guarantee on human-agreement rate at a chosen coverage level.

AVRL differs in three ways. First, Trust or Escalate operates at evaluation time on a fixed, already-trained policy; AVRL's allocator operates during RL training, where the policy and the proxy's exploitability are non-stationary and can drift toward regions the proxy scores incorrectly, something the escalation rule was never designed to detect. Second, Trust or Escalate's escalation signal is the judge's own stated confidence; AVRL's allocator is proposed to learn where in prompt space the proxy is exploitable, for example correlating with problem type, difficulty, or self-consistency spread, a structural signal closer in spirit to SOFisher's spatial autocorrelation than to judge self-confidence. Third, Trust or Escalate optimizes a human-agreement guarantee on a single evaluation instance; AVRL is proposed to optimize a per-rollout-group, budget-constrained decision inside the RL loop, which must also track and suppress corrupted-reward propagation into the policy gradient, not just re-score a single judgment.
