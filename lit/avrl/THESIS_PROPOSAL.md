# Thesis Proposal

## Title

Verification-Aware Reinforcement Learning: Learning When to Pay for Ground Truth

## Abstract

Reinforcement learning post-training of language models (RLVR, GRPO, RLAIF) scores every rollout with a verifier: an LLM judge, a code sandbox, a human, or a long tool run. This step is usually the dominant cost and the main reward-hacking surface, yet no published system decides, online and jointly with the policy being trained, whether a rollout group deserves the expensive verifier or a cheap proxy such as majority vote. Stated honestly, the contribution here is narrow: verifier-choice allocation learned online inside the reward-scoring step of RLVR, evaluated on corrupted-reward admission, since the allocator mechanism and the fixed verify-or-proxy decision already exist separately (see Contributions and Problem Statement). This thesis proposes AVRL (verification-aware reinforcement learning): a learned, budget-capped allocator that routes each rollout group to the expensive verifier or a cheap proxy, trains online from the verified subset it chooses to inspect, and is evaluated not just on accuracy per dollar but on how many corrupted reward labels it lets into the policy gradient. The central empirical claim, following the mechanistic precedent of SOFisher (Li et al., 2026, Nature Communications, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73404-6), is that a learned allocator beats a periodically recalibrated cost-stratified threshold at equal expensive-call budget because proxy error clusters in prompt space in a way two cheap pre-training statistics predict in advance. The thesis is designed to give a clear, pre-registered answer either way: if the structure is not there, or does not predict the allocator's gain, the honest result is a measurement of proxy-error geometry plus a recalibrated-threshold recipe.

## Problem Statement

RL post-training treats rollout generation, reward scoring, and the policy update as separate cost centers (DAPO, Yu et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476; HybridFlow/verl, Sheng et al., 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19256; OpenRLHF, Hu et al., 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11143). No system reviewed discloses a direct dollar or GPU-hour split between these costs; this premise is inferred from engineering choices, not confirmed accounting (UNVERIFIED in that specific form). What is documented is that verification is expensive and gameable: almost any imperfect proxy reward is hackable in principle (Skalse, Howe, Krasheninnikov, Krueger, 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085), hacking concentrates in identifiable task clusters (METR, von Arx, Chan, Barnes, 2025, https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/, 39 of 128 RE-Bench runs, concentrated in one category), and a policy that can infer a monitor's pattern learns to hide hacking from it rather than stop (Baker et al., OpenAI, 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926).

Existing systems handle only part of this problem. Trust or Escalate (Jung, Brahman, Choi, ICLR 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370) and Heteroskedastic Signals (Yang, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841) route between cheap and expensive scoring, but both are fixed rules calibrated once at evaluation time, not learned online inside a training loop where the policy and its failure modes keep changing. VIP (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601) and SARA (Nomand et al., 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26253) learn budget-capped, in-training allocation, but for rollout or token count, not verifier choice, and neither measures corrupted rewards admitted into training. SOFisher (Li et al., 2026, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73404-6) is a non-adversarial version of the idea outside language models: a double-DQN policy choosing which field of view to image next under a budget, exploiting spatial autocorrelation of rare targets for roughly 5x sampling efficiency over random sampling, with no equivalent of a policy that can learn to game the measurement. No retrieved paper does all of: (a) choose expensive-verifier-or-proxy per rollout group, (b) under a hard cap on expensive calls, (c) learned online during training, and (d) evaluated on corrupted-reward admission. OpenFC (Wang, Qiu, Lin, Lv, Chen, Wang, Yang, Zhang, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33579) also uses the name "verification-aware reinforcement learning" for a budget-aware, RL-trained tool-call gate, but on agentic fact-checking tool calls during generation, not reward-scoring of completed rollouts in RLVR; the closest terminological neighbor, distinguished here rather than left uncited.

## Three Contributions

1. Verifier-choice allocation learned online inside the reward-scoring step of RLVR, evaluated on corrupted-reward admission: stated honestly, since the allocator mechanism (VIP, SARA) and the verify-or-proxy decision (Trust or Escalate, Heteroskedastic Signals) each already exist separately but have not been combined inside one training loop or measured this way.
2. An evaluation protocol centered on corrupted-reward admission rate at equal expensive-call budget, a metric no cited baseline reports, alongside the standard accuracy-per-dollar view.
3. A predictive, falsifiable account, not just a detection, of when proxy error clusters in prompt space: two measurable pre-training statistics, the neighbor-hit ratio of proxy error over embedding cells and the AUC of cheap features for proxy error (both already implemented in avrl/p0_analyze.py), with the pre-registered prediction that the learned allocator's gain over a recalibrated threshold is monotone in that statistic across task and proxy pairs, the thesis's central claim, not a Phase 0 side check. RewardBench (Lambert et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13787) and Gao, Schulman, Hilton (2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760) establish that proxy error is structured, but neither predicts when an allocator can exploit that structure.

## Method

**State features.** Each rollout group is described by cheap, gold-free features from the group's own responses: majority-vote agreement fraction, number of distinct final answers, mean and standard deviation of response length, fraction truncated, and query length, concatenated with a low-dimensional PCA projection of prompt or response embeddings (21 features currently). No gold label enters this vector.

**Policy class.** The allocator scores each candidate group with an online logistic regression over these features, predicting the probability that the cheap proxy disagrees with the expensive verifier. That probability seeds a Beta prior for a Thompson-sampling layer keyed on the embedding-derived cluster ("cell") each group falls into; groups draw an independent sample from their cell's posterior, and the top-k by sampled score go to the expensive verifier. Before the first fit, the allocator falls back to a fixed low-agreement-first rule (avrl/allocators.py).

**Budget accounting.** A single pure function turns (group count, budget fraction) into an integer expensive-call count per step, no carry-over, so every arm at the same budget spends exactly the same number of expensive calls.

**Baselines.** The primary baseline is a periodically recalibrated cost-stratified threshold: the same low-agreement-first cut rule, but refit on the same cumulative verified-sample set the allocator sees, at the same cadence, rather than fixed once before training; this answers the cannot-work concern that a fixed-once threshold is too easy a target. A second baseline, VIP-transfer, re-applies VIP's Gaussian-process-plus-convex-optimization allocation (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601) with verify-yes/no as the resource in place of rollout count, testing whether any gap comes from the allocation axis or VIP's specific machinery.

**Online learning from verified samples.** The regression is refit periodically on the cumulative set of groups the allocator itself chose to verify; the proxy-error label is gold-vs-majority mismatch, available only for verified groups. Because the training set is always a subset the allocator selected, this creates a selection-bias risk that must be reported alongside results: an allocator that stops looking where it predicts low error could blind itself to drift there (risk 2 in the synthesis; Baker et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926).

**Use of prompt-space structure.** The embedding-derived cells are the functional analog of SOFisher's spatial coordinates: SOFisher exploits spatial autocorrelation of rare targets on a tissue slide, AVRL exploits clustering of proxy error in embedding space. Phase 0 tests this directly: do embedding neighborhoods predict proxy error beyond cheap scalar features.

## Experimental Plan

**Phase 0, diagnostic (goldberg, RTX 5090).** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, 3,000 GSM8K training prompts, 8 rollouts per prompt. Cheap proxy is majority-vote agreement; expensive verifier is the gold answer. Measures: overall and confident-group proxy-error rate, AUC of cheap features versus embeddings for predicting proxy error, embedding-neighborhood error ratio, and a replay of random, threshold, learned, and oracle allocation at 10, 25, and 50 percent verification budgets, scored on proxy errors caught per verifier call. No literature baseline runs here; this phase establishes the premise downstream baselines depend on.

**Phase 1, training loop (hannah, 4x A100 PCIe 40GB).** GRPO with LoRA on the same model, 400 steps, five arms at a fixed 25 percent verification budget, each run across 3 seeds: full (verify every group, upper bound), random, threshold (the recalibrated rule defined above, the closest reproducible analog to Heteroskedastic Signals' thresholding, Yang, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841), VIP-transfer (defined above), and learned (this thesis's allocator). The zero-expensive-call extreme (proxy-only, in the spirit of TTRL's majority-vote-only reward, Zuo et al., 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16084, roughly a 211 percent relative pass@1 gain on AIME 2024 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B) is logged as a reference point, not a budget-matched competitor. Logged per step: expensive calls spent, corrupted-reward groups admitted (gold used for logging only outside the "full" arm), and greedy accuracy on 500 held-out prompts every 50 steps. The learned-versus-threshold gap is tracked at every checkpoint, per the decision rule in Kill Criteria below.

**Phase 2, generalization (hannah, contingent on Phase 1 surviving).** Centerpiece, not a late ablation: test contribution 3's prediction across at least four task/proxy pairs (GSM8K and MATH-500 under majority-vote and small-judge proxies, per Phase 0's task/proxy variation). For each pair, fit the allocator and the recalibrated threshold, record the neighbor-hit ratio and cheap-feature AUC, measure the allocator's gain over the threshold, and test whether that gain is monotone in the statistic, as pre-registered. Separately, test whether a GSM8K-fitted allocator's edge survives zero-shot transfer to an unseen pair without retraining. Secondary items follow: a real expensive judge (7B or API model) on open-ended and code tasks with a small reward-model proxy, allocator and policy trained jointly, and whether concentrating verification where the proxy is exploitable reduces measured reward hacking. Trust or Escalate (cited above) becomes a directly comparable evaluation-time baseline here; VIP and SARA (cited above) run as a different-axis ablation, since both allocate rollout count rather than verifier choice.

## Evaluation Metrics

- Held-out accuracy at equal expensive-call budget (primary metric, Phase 1 and 2).
- Corrupted-reward admission rate: fraction of reward labels used in the policy gradient that disagree with gold, at matched budget (no cited baseline reports this).
- Proxy errors caught per verifier call at fixed budget (Phase 0 replay).
- AUC of cheap features alone versus cheap features plus embeddings for predicting proxy error, plus embedding-neighborhood error ratio (Phase 0).
- Correlation between the two structure statistics (neighbor-hit ratio, cheap-feature AUC) and the allocator's gain over the recalibrated threshold, across task/proxy pairs (Phase 2 centerpiece).
- Reward-hacking incidence by task category (Phase 2), reported the way METR reports RE-Bench hacking concentration (von Arx, Chan, Barnes, 2025, https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/).
- Cross-task-family transfer accuracy (Phase 2).

## Compute Plan

Phase 0 runs on goldberg, a local RTX 5090 (32GB) machine, for vLLM rollout generation and diagnostic analysis; sufficient because Phase 0 is inference only, no training. Phase 1 and Phase 2 require hannah, a 4x A100 PCIe (40GB) cluster, for GRPO/LoRA training; hannah jobs are capped at 3 days, so Phase 1's runs across 5 arms and 3 seeds are budgeted as sequential or job-array submissions within that cap. Phase 2's real-judge API cost is unestimated, an open item to price before Phase 2 begins.

## Kill Criteria Per Phase

**Phase 0.** If proxy error among confident groups (agreement at or above 0.75) is under 3 percent, budgeting is moot on this task; move to a harder task or noisier proxy before Phase 1. If embeddings add less than 0.05 AUC over cheap features and the neighborhood error ratio is under 1.5x, claim (3) is dead on this task; contributions 1 and 2 remain but must be won with a fixed rule, not a learned one.

**Phase 1.** Pre-registered decision rule (from the cannot-work gap check): track the learned-minus-recalibrated-threshold accuracy gap at every 50-step checkpoint. A flat or shrinking gap across training steps kills the claim, the expected signature if the allocator's selection-bias blind spot outgrows its edge as training proceeds. A gap that holds or grows beyond 3-seed variance at the final step supports the claim. If killed, the recalibrated-threshold recipe becomes the deliverable instead of the learned allocator.

**Phase 2.** If the Phase 1 advantage does not replicate once the verifier is a real judge, or the allocator's own selection bias measurably blinds it to drift, the honest result is that in-training allocation works only in the gold-label regime; reported as a scope-limited positive result rather than pursued further.

## Expected Paper

Two outcomes are pre-registered; either is a complete result. If the allocator clears both kill tests, the paper reports a learned, budget-capped, in-training verification allocator that beats a recalibrated threshold and a VIP-machinery transfer baseline at equal expensive-call budget, admits fewer corrupted rewards, and attributes the edge to a predictive account of proxy-error clustering that holds across task and proxy pairs, not post hoc, positioned against VIP, SARA, Trust or Escalate, Heteroskedastic Signals, and OpenFC as the first system moving this decision from evaluation-time, rollout-count, or tool-call allocation to in-training verifier-choice allocation inside RLVR reward scoring. If not, the paper reports a negative result in the shape of Heteroskedastic Signals: a recalibrated rule already captures most of the achievable gain, plus the Phase 0/Phase 2 characterization of proxy-error clustering across task and proxy pairs, a measurement not previously reported. Target venue either way: a training-efficiency or RL-post-training track (ICLR, NeurIPS, or a relevant workshop).
