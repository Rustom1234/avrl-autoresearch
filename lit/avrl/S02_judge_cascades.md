# S02: LLM-as-a-judge reliability and cost-aware judging (2023-2026)

## 1. Contribution to the AVRL thesis

This angle supplies the empirical base rate for how unreliable and how expensive verification actually is, which is the premise AVRL needs before proposing to allocate it. Judge-bias and judge-agreement work shows that both the cheap proxy and the expensive verifier are noisy and exploitable in different, partially predictable ways, which is what makes a learned allocator (rather than a fixed rule) plausible. Cascade and selective-evaluation work is the direct technical ancestor of AVRL's core mechanism, having already shown that escalation decisions can be made with statistical guarantees and that routing policy, not just judge choice, is where the budget savings live.

## 2. Sources

- Jung, Brahman, Choi, 2024 (ICLR 2025), arXiv:2407.18370, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370: Cascaded Selective Evaluation with Mistral-7B as first-stage judge reaches over 80% human agreement at close to 80% test coverage on a Chatbot Arena subset where GPT-4 alone cannot hit 80% agreement.
- Yang, 2026, arXiv:2606.15841, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841: cost-stratified thresholding beats a single static threshold by up to 17 percentage points in hit rate when uncertainty quality is heteroskedastic across cost tiers; Heterogeneity-Gated Allocation recovers most of this gain via a comparability test, only stratifying when it helps.
- Nguyen, Nguyen, Ma, Zhao, She, Nguyen, ICLR 2026, arXiv:2602.01601, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601: VIP uses a Gaussian process over success probability plus convex optimization to allocate rollouts per prompt under a fixed budget, beating uniform and heuristic allocation (the closest existing "learned allocator" to AVRL, but for rollout count, not verifier choice).

- 2024, "Hybrid LLM: Cost-Efficient and Quality-Aware Query Routing," arXiv:2404.14618, https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14618: a learned router between a small and a large LLM cuts calls to the expensive model while preserving output quality; same allocator logic as AVRL but applied to generation, not verification.
- 2025, "Select-then-Route: Taxonomy-Guided Routing for LLMs," https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-industry.28: industry-track evidence that routing policy benefits from structure over the input taxonomy rather than confidence alone, supporting the "prompt-space structure" half of the AVRL claim.
- 2024, "Disentangling Length from Quality in Direct Preference Optimization," https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.297: shows DPO/RLHF reward signals derived from judges conflate length with quality, a concrete, measured case of an exploitable judge-derived reward.
- 2024, "Humans or LLMs as the Judge? A Study on Judgement Biases," arXiv:2402.10669, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10669: finds both human and LLM judges carry distinct, non-overlapping biases, undermining any assumption that either one is a clean ground truth to calibrate against.

## 3. Support versus threat to the thesis

Supporting the claim: Trust or Escalate already proves that a cascade with a statistical escalation rule can match a strong judge's reliability at a fraction of the expensive-judge calls, which is the static-cascade floor AVRL must beat. The Heteroskedastic Signals paper (arXiv:2606.15841) is the strongest direct support: it shows static thresholds leave up to 17 points of hit-rate on the table specifically because verification difficulty is heterogeneous across cost strata, which is exactly the structure a learned, per-group allocator is positioned to exploit rather than a single fixed cutoff. The bias literature (verbosity, position, self-preference, length-quality conflation) collectively establishes that cheap proxies fail in identifiable, clustered ways rather than randomly, which is the empirical precondition for "learns where the proxy is exploitable" to be a meaningful claim rather than an assumption.

Threatening the claim: the Heteroskedastic Signals paper cuts both ways. Its own headline is that structural heterogeneity "limits optimization gains," meaning a more sophisticated allocator may still hit a ceiling once the comparability test says stratification is not helping, so AVRL needs to show its learned policy clears that ceiling, not just matches HGA. Trust or Escalate already delivers provable guarantees with a simple, cheap, non-learned escalation rule; if a fixed or lightly-tuned threshold gets within a small margin of a learned allocator at equal expensive-call budget, the complexity and instability of training an allocator jointly with the policy may not be justified, particularly given RL training's own instability. The weak-judges-judging-strong-models result (arXiv:2407.04622) is a caution rather than support: it shows outcomes depend heavily on protocol structure (debate versus consultancy, task type), which means the allocator's gains could be protocol-specific and not transfer cleanly across task domains the way the thesis implies.

## 4. Closest prior work from this angle

Trust or Escalate (Jung, Brahman, Choi, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2407.18370) is the closest work in this angle, and arguably the closest work to AVRL overall alongside the two already-named cascade papers. It differs from AVRL in three ways. First, it is a fixed cascade with a confidence-based escalation rule calibrated once via Simulated Annotators, not a policy learned jointly with, or adapting during, RL training, so it cannot shift its escalation behavior as the policy distribution changes over training steps. Second, its objective is a provable human-agreement guarantee at evaluation time, a one-shot judging setting, whereas AVRL targets in-training reward allocation across a GRPO/RLVR loop where the cost of an admitted corrupted reward compounds through policy updates rather than being a single mislabeled test example. Third, it does not model or exploit prompt-space clustering of proxy error as a first-class learned signal, it escalates based on judge confidence on the current instance, not on where in prompt space the proxy is known to be unreliable, which is the specific mechanism AVRL proposes to add.
