# K02: Adversarial Prior-Art Kill Attempt on H2 (Learned Data Curriculum)

## Three closest works

1. **Group-MATES**, 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14709. Partitions the corpus into small clusters via a relational influence model trained on oracle influences from training trajectories, selects within each cluster. Result: 3.5-9.4% relative gain over random across 22 downstream tasks, up to 1.75x fewer tokens to target performance, 400M-3B params on DCLM. Document-cluster granularity, oracle-derived signals, beats domain-level baselines, but one-shot/static, not a sequential bandit/RL policy. UNVERIFIED: exact cluster count and peer-review status.

2. **MATES** (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06046. Periodically retrained small influence model scores documents against oracle probes. Doubles the gain of the prior best reference-model selection method, halves FLOPs to target performance, 410M/1B on C4. Document-level, densely applied, no rationing of when to pay for the expensive signal.

3. **Aioli** (Chen et al., ICLR 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05735. Unifies prior online mixing methods as an online-fit mixing law, domain granularity (single digits to tens of arms). Beats stratified sampling on 6/6 datasets by 0.27 perplexity points average; prior online mixers were up to 6.9pp worse than stratified. Near-zero overhead, no expensive probes.

## Strongest "already done" argument

Group-MATES already delivers H2's core sell, cluster-granularity data selection informed by influence/oracle signals beating domain-level baselines, via a **static** one-shot fit, no online policy. This hits H2's own kill criterion 4: if domain-level granularity captures most of the gain, the active-search design isn't doing the work. Group-MATES is the inverse experiment: large granularity payoff (1.75x token reduction) **without** a sequential policy. A committee could argue the RL machinery solves a problem (when to pay for the expensive signal) not yet shown to matter once a good static cluster-level selector exists.

## Judgment

already_done leans high: the granularity claim, the cheap-plus-expensive-signal pattern, and domain-level online policies are each independently demonstrated with real numbers. Unpublished: the triple combination of sequential/online policy, cluster granularity, and rationed expensive probes, compared against Group-MATES as a static cluster baseline rather than only Aioli/ADO. That comparison is the narrowest surviving gap. The thesis's own baseline list (Section 5) names only Aioli, never Group-MATES, which weakens its claim of testing against the strongest existing competitor.
