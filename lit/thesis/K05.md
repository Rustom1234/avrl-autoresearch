# K05: Kill Hunt on H5 (Budgeted Memorisation and Extraction Auditing)

## Three closest works

1. **Biderman et al. 2025, Hubble: A Model Suite to Advance the Study of LLM Memorization.** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19811 (also https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19811). Public suite of 8 models (1B and 8B params, 100B or 500B tokens) with controlled, injected memorisation targets (book passages, biographies, benchmark items) across checkpoints. Shows memorisation depends on target frequency relative to corpus size, and that unrepeated sensitive data can be forgotten without continued exposure. This already delivers the exact injected-target, multi-checkpoint testbed the thesis budgets months 1-3 to build (H05 section 5, Phase 1).

2. **ACL BlackboxNLP 2024, Insights from Tracking LLM Memorization During Training.** https://aclanthology.org/2024.blackboxnlp-1.4.pdf. Tracks held-in vs held-out log-likelihood across Pythia checkpoints, finding a memorisation spike after LR warm-up with long persistence in larger models. This is passive, full-coverage tracking on a known set, not a budgeted policy, but it already delivers "continuous across checkpoints" monitoring that H05 lists as a Phase 2/3 contribution.

3. **Xujun Che, Depeng Xu, Shuhan Yuan 2026, Memorization Is Not Extraction: Tight Differential-Privacy Bounds and Audit Blind Spots.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27782 UNVERIFIED exact venue/date (arxiv id suggests 2026). Proves that "every adaptive extraction protocol with list budget m" succeeds with probability at most 1-f(kappa) under f-DP, i.e. a formal ceiling on any adaptive querying policy, and shows memorization and extractability can diverge (one mechanism memorised-but-unextractable, another extractable-but-score-invisible). This directly targets the theoretical premise that a smarter policy can meaningfully outperform static probing, at least under DP-trained models.

Also relevant: Nasr et al. 2023 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17035, $200 query budget, 10,000+ examples recovered via a fixed divergence attack, not adaptive) and Kandpal et al. 2022 (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/kandpal22a/kandpal22a.pdf, regeneration rate roughly 1000x higher for 10x-duplicated vs single-seen sequences) — both already in H05's own prior-art list.

## Strongest reason a committee would object

No single paper runs the full loop (online bandit/RL policy, updates on hits, persists across checkpoints, beats static duplication-rank). But the pieces a committee would flag: Hubble already supplies the injected-target/checkpoint infrastructure, and Kandpal 2022 shows duplication count predicts regeneration near-deterministically at the superlinear scaling level. That means a static duplication-rank baseline is already a strong oracle-like proxy, so the marginal lift a learned policy adds may be small, which is literally H05's own kill criterion (need greater than 15% more hits than static ranking). The 2608.27782 DP-bound result adds a theoretical ceiling on adaptive protocols generally, reinforcing that the "policy learns something ranking cannot" bet is the single load-bearing, unproven assumption.

## Judgment

already_done is moderate, not total: the testbed and the static baseline both pre-exist, but the actual adaptive-policy-vs-static-ranking comparison, run as a continuous checkpoint monitor, has not been published. The surviving gap is narrow and is exactly the gap H05 already targets with its kill criterion, which is a good sign methodologically but a bad sign for "genuinely new" scope.
