# K18: Adversarial Prior-Art Hunt on H18 (Budgeted Semantic Dedup)

Search note: 5-minute budget. Semantic Scholar returned 429 on every query; abstracts came from WebFetch on arxiv abstract pages instead.

## Three closest works found

1. **SEDD: Scalable and Efficient Dataset Deduplication with GPUs** (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01046 - GPU MinHash-LSH pipeline deduplicating 1.2 trillion tokens in 3 hours on 32 V100 GPUs, 158x faster than CPU SlimPajama dedup, 7.8x faster than NVIDIA NeMo Curator. Closest work: a direct cost analysis of dedup at trillion-token scale, the axis H18's "budget" premise depends on.
2. **LSHBloom: Memory-efficient, Extreme-scale Document Deduplication** (2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04257 - replaces MinHashLSH's index with Bloom filters: 18x less disk, 12x faster on peS2o, extrapolated to "several billion documents."
3. **SPOKES: Optimizing for Diverse Pretraining Data Selection** (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15216 - a diversification method (G-Vendi score, exponentiated gradient descent) beating semantic dedup as a baseline outright: +1.5 points on DCLM, +1.4 on FineWeb, "outperforming all baselines, including semantic deduplication and quality filtering."

## What these do not do

None builds a sequential, learned policy deciding where to spend expensive embedding-dedup compute, conditioned on prior-pass results. SEDD and LSHBloom optimize the cheap tier, not allocation of the expensive tier. SPOKES replaces dedup with a different objective instead of allocating dedup compute adaptively. This matches H18's own note that no cost-aware allocation paper was found.

## Strongest reason a committee could attack this

SEDD shows the cheap-hashing tier is now essentially free at trillion-token scale (3 hours on 32 GPUs versus weeks of training), which H18 already assumed. The sharper threat is SPOKES: semantic dedup as a uniform pass is already beaten outright by a diversification objective needing no budget rationing at all. If diversification dominates dedup with no allocation machinery, a committee could argue H18 is optimizing allocation of a lever a simpler alternative already beats, capping the policy's ceiling regardless of allocation quality. Neither paper reports actual SemDeDup/D4 embedding cost at trillion-token scale relative to pretraining FLOPs, the load-bearing number behind "there is a scarce budget." UNVERIFIED: that cost ratio.

## Judgment

The exact gap H18 claims is not hit by these three or by H18's own search. But the premise is under pressure: cheap-tier cost keeps falling toward zero (SEDD, LSHBloom), and SPOKES shows dedup is already beatable by a non-dedup alternative. A narrower gap survives: adaptive embedding-budget allocation beating both (a) uniform SemDeDup/D4 at matched budget and (b) diversification-only methods like SPOKES. Phase 1's kill criteria cover (a) but not (b); add SPOKES-style diversification as a required baseline.
