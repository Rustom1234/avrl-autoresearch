# K14: Adversarial prior-art hunt on H14 (budgeted repeated exposure)

## Verification of the two named papers

1. "Train Smarter, Not Longer: Memorization-Guided Data Reuse for Efficient LLM Training," Zuo et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04969. Confirmed. Submitted July 6, 2026, published at the 3rd DATA-FM workshop @ ICLR 2026. Uses a "Memorization Window" signal from loss-retention dynamics and downstream eval scores to adaptively decide when/how much to reuse data and how to schedule replays. This is a workshop paper with preliminary results, not a peer-reviewed main-track paper.

2. "When to Review: Spaced Repetition for Continual Pre-Training of Language Models," Atreya et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17530. Confirmed. Submitted August 18, 2026. Uses SuperMemo-2 (SM-2) to schedule replay of previously-seen examples during continual pretraining, targeting catastrophic forgetting after a distribution shift, not first-pass data-constrained repetition scheduling. H14's own framing of this distinction (item 7) is accurate.

## One more close work found

"Internal Data Repetition Destroys Language Models," Chudnovsky, Kazdan, Levi, Schaeffer et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24998, submitted June 23, 2026. Finds repetition damage peaks at an intermediate repeat count and that peak follows a power law in model size, with 10% repeated data causing compute losses equivalent to 67% of total FLOPs in some configurations. Analytical/measurement paper (misspecified linear regression model), not a scheduling policy: it does not propose or test any method for deciding which corpus regions to repeat. UNVERIFIED whether its "exact-document repetition" setup transfers to H14's region-level (shard/topic) repetition unit.

## Strongest reason a committee would say "already done" or "cannot work"

H14's own section 4 already concedes the weakest leg: Zuo et al. 2026 (item 8, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04969) shows a hand-built, cheap loss-retention heuristic already captures a meaningful chunk of the achievable gain from adaptive repetition scheduling. That is the core empirical claim H14 needs a full RL/bandit policy to establish value beyond a heuristic, and the closest existing paper already demonstrates the heuristic alone works reasonably well. A committee's strongest objection: the interesting scientific claim ("repetition value is heterogeneous across corpus regions and can be captured by cheap signals") is already published; what remains is whether a learned policy beats a tuned heuristic by enough to justify a year, which is an engineering delta, not a new hypothesis. This is compounded by arXiv:2606.24998, which shows repetition damage is non-monotonic and scale-dependent (power law in model size), meaning a 125M-parameter Phase 1 pilot (as planned in H14 section 5) may sit in a damage/benefit regime that does not predict behavior at 350M-1B, undermining the plan to gate Phase 2 on Phase 1 results alone.

## Judgment

The thesis retains a narrow surviving gap. No paper found treats "which region gets its Nth exposure next" as a sequential decision problem with an explicit learned policy compared against both uniform epoching and the Zuo et al. heuristic at matched tokens, and no paper measures autocorrelation of cheap per-region signals directly. But H14's own kill criteria (section 6) already pre-concede that if the heuristic captures most of the gain, the project should downgrade from "learned policy" to "adopt the heuristic" -- which is close to what arXiv:2607.04969 already reports. A full year scoped around proving RL adds value over a heuristic that already works is a thin thesis; the defensible core is narrower than the SOFisher-style framing suggests.
