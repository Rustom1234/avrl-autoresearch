# H10: Sequential, Model-in-the-Loop Annotation for Data-Quality and Safety Filters

## 1. Hypothesis and SOFisher mapping

Sequential annotation, where a cheap classifier is refit on labels collected so far and used to pick the next documents to send to an expensive annotator (strong LLM or human), finds more rare positives per annotation dollar than the standard practice of annotating a fixed random sample once and distilling a classifier from it. If true, filtered corpora built this way should match or beat budget-matched random-annotation corpora on rare-label recall (toxicity, licensed text, contamination, high educational value) at equal annotation spend, and should train equal or better downstream models.

SOFisher maps as: field of view -> candidate document; expensive fluorescence imaging -> strong-model or human label; cheap state features -> embeddings or a small classifier's current score; spatial autocorrelation of rare targets on a slide -> co-occurrence of rare labels among documents that are close in embedding/feature space. The DQN's job (pick the next FOV given what has been imaged so far) becomes: pick the next document to annotate given the classifier fit on labels so far.

## 2. Prior art

- FineWeb-Edu / FineWeb: 460k random FineWeb documents scored 0-5 for educational value by Llama-3-70B-Instruct; a linear regressor head on a small encoder is fit on ~410k of these; threshold >=3 keeps ~8% of the corpus. Random single-pass annotation, no active loop. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17557
- DCLM (DataComp-LM): fastText classifier trained on OpenHermes-2.5 + ELI5 positives vs. web negatives, ranks documents by predicted probability, keeps top ~10%; fastText filtering gives ~3.5-point Core-metric gain over reference-only filtering. Random-labeled reference sets, no active loop. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11794
- QuRating (Wettig et al., ICML 2024): LLM pairwise judgments over 4 quality axes distilled into a "QuRater" scoring model, used to annotate a 260B-token corpus; 1.3B models trained on 30B selected tokens. Random pair sampling for training the rater. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09739
- Ask-LLM (Sachdeva et al., "How to Train Data-Efficient LLMs", ICLR 2026): zero-shot LLM quality judgment per example beats perplexity/heuristic filters; training on the top ~10% selected this way matches or beats full-data training with up to 70% faster convergence. Selection is score-and-rank on a fixed labeled/scored pool, not sequential re-annotation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09668
- Settles, "Active Learning Literature Survey" (2009): canonical survey of query strategies (uncertainty, query-by-committee, expected model change) underlying all pool-based active learning used below. https://burrsettles.com/pub/settles.activelearning.pdf
- Garnett et al., "Bayesian Optimal Active Search and Surveying" (ICML 2012): introduces active search, a decision-theoretic formulation for finding members of a rare valuable class rather than minimizing overall generalization error; directly analogous to hunting rare toxicity/contamination positives. https://icml.cc/2012/papers/618.pdf
- Jiang et al., "Efficient Nonmyopic Active Search" (ICML 2017): nonmyopic lookahead policies for active search, closer in spirit to a learned multi-step policy than one-step uncertainty sampling. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jiang17d/jiang17d.pdf
- GALAXY (2022): graph-based active learning for extreme/imbalanced multi-class settings, explicitly targets rare-class discovery in large label spaces. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01402
- "Enhancing Text Classification through LLM-Driven Active Learning and Human Annotation" (ACL LAW 2024): hybrid LLM+human active learning loop for text classification, reports lower annotation cost at matched/improved accuracy (exact numbers UNVERIFIED from search snippet). https://aclanthology.org/2024.law-1.10/
- "From Selection to Generation: A Survey of LLM-based Active Learning" (2025): taxonomy of LLM-in-the-loop active learning, covers using LLMs as both selector and oracle. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11767
- "Reassessing Active Learning Adoption in Contemporary NLP" (2025): community survey on why active learning is underused in modern NLP pipelines despite gains in academic studies. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.09701
- "LLM on a Budget: Active Knowledge Distillation for Efficient Classification of Large Text Corpora" (2025): combines active learning with LLM-teacher distillation (M-RARU sampling) to cut labeling cost for a student classifier; direct precedent for sequential LLM-in-the-loop distillation of a corpus-scale classifier. https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11574v1
- Performance-Guided Knowledge Distillation (Amazon Science, 2024): active-learning loop between a student text classifier and an LLM teacher with hard-negative mining for production-scale classification. https://www.amazon.science/publications/performance-guided-llm-knowledge-distillation-for-efficient-text-classification-at-scale
- Burns et al., "Weak-to-Strong Generalization" (OpenAI, 2023): strong models finetuned on weak-model labels outperform the weak supervisor; relevant baseline logic for why a cheap classifier's picks, even if imperfect, can bootstrap a better filter. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09390
- "Towards Safer Pretraining: Analyzing and Filtering Harmful Content in Webscale Datasets" (2025): GPT-4o-labeled toxicity data (HarmFormer classifier) at webscale; single-pass random-labeled construction, the closest existing toxicity-filter analog to FineWeb-Edu's pipeline. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02009
- SOFisher (Ng et al., Nature Communications, 2026): DQN policy for next-FOV selection in spatial omics imaging under a fixed imaging budget, the source analogy for this thesis. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73404-6

## 3. What is genuinely open as of 2026

No published pipeline runs a closed active-annotation loop (refit classifier -> pick next batch -> call strong LLM/human -> refit) for corpus-scale pretraining filters. FineWeb-Edu, DCLM, and QuRating all fix the annotated sample once, at random, before distillation; Ask-LLM scores a fixed pool rather than sequentially choosing what to send to the LLM oracle. LLM-in-the-loop active learning (2024-2025 surveys above) exists for downstream text classification but has not been evaluated on: (a) genuinely rare pretraining-filter labels (contamination, licensed text, severe toxicity, which run well under 1% base rate, versus the more balanced tasks in those papers), (b) budgets as small as 3-10% of the FineWeb-Edu-scale label count, or (c) whether the resulting filtered corpus changes downstream model quality or safety, not just classifier AUC. The researcher's own FineWeb result (~2x rare positives at 3-10% budget) is a single task, single corpus, unreplicated on other label types, and untested end-to-end through model training. A year is needed because: extending from one label (educational value proxy) to 3-4 heterogeneous rare-label types, running the loop at corpus scale with real strong-model calls (cost and latency), and training and evaluating downstream models on the resulting filtered corpora (the actual claim, "trains better or safer models") are each multi-month efforts, and the safety-relevant labels (toxicity, contamination) need careful eval design that doesn't exist off the shelf.

## 4. Three-condition check

1. Rare labels are autocorrelated in cheap-feature space. Evidence: the researcher's preliminary FineWeb result itself (self-training annotator finds ~2x rare positives at matched budget) is direct evidence a cheap classifier's residual uncertainty/score correlates with true rarity; QuRating and Ask-LLM both show LLM-quality-style labels are predictable from cheap encoder features well enough to build a scoring model at all (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09739, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09668).
2. Expensive measurement costs far more than cheap features. Evidence: FineWeb-Edu calls a 70B model on 460k documents once and then scores 15T tokens with a small frozen-encoder linear head (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17557); DCLM's fastText scoring is near-free versus fastText's own labeled-set construction cost (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11794). The cost asymmetry that makes SOFisher work (imaging >> state features) holds directly for LLM/human labels versus classifier inference.
3. Cheap features alone cannot rank targets well. Evidence: Ask-LLM shows LLM-based judgments beat perplexity and n-gram heuristics for selection (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09668); QuRating shows pairwise LLM judgment captures quality axes that simple heuristics miss (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09739); if cheap features sufficed, none of FineWeb-Edu/DCLM/QuRating would need a strong-model annotation pass at all.

## 5. Year plan

Phase 1 (weeks 1-4, 4 A100s, under a week of compute): extend the FineWeb self-training-annotator result to toxicity and licensed-text labels. Reuse the FineWeb-Edu pipeline scaffolding (encoder + linear head), swap the label source for a toxicity oracle (e.g., strong-LLM prompted per the HarmFormer/"Towards Safer Pretraining" setup, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02009) and a licensed-text oracle (LLM or metadata heuristic), add an uncertainty-sampling baseline (Settles-style margin/entropy sampling, https://burrsettles.com/pub/settles.activelearning.pdf) alongside random and self-training-active selection. Metric: rare-positive recall at matched annotation budget (3%, 10%), and AUPRC of the resulting classifier on a held-out random sample. Baseline to beat: budget-matched random-sample distillation in the style of FineWeb-Edu/DCLM (strongest deployed baseline), with uncertainty sampling as the second bar since it is the standard active-learning default.

Phase 2 (months 2-6): scale to a real corpus-filtering run (FineWeb-scale slice), add active search-style nonmyopic selection (Garnett 2012, Jiang 2017) versus one-step uncertainty, test 3-4 label types together, and produce two filtered corpora (random-budget vs. sequential-budget) at matched annotation dollars.

Phase 3 (months 6-12): train matched-size LMs (or continue-pretrain) on each filtered corpus, evaluate on standard benchmarks plus safety/contamination-specific evals, and write up whether sequential annotation's classifier-level gains survive through to model-level gains.

## 6. Kill criteria

Kill after Phase 1 if the self-training annotator's rare-positive advantage does not replicate (falls under 1.3x random at matched budget) on at least one of toxicity or licensed-text, or if uncertainty sampling alone captures most of the gain (sequential model-in-the-loop adds no advantage over plain active learning). Kill after Phase 2 if classifier-level gains do not survive at corpus scale with real (noisy, rate-limited) strong-model calls. Kill after Phase 3 if downstream model quality/safety metrics are statistically indistinguishable between the two filtered corpora despite classifier-level differences, since that would mean the whole thesis claim (better filters -> better models) does not hold even though the annotation-efficiency claim does.

## 7. Impact

A lab that adopts this would stop drawing its LLM/human-annotation sample uniformly at random before distilling a filter classifier, and instead run annotation as an iterative loop: annotate a small seed batch, fit/refit the cheap classifier, use it to pick the next batch (biased toward likely-rare-positive or high-uncertainty documents), repeat until budget exhausted, then distill the final filter from all collected labels. Concretely this changes the FineWeb-Edu/DCLM/QuRating-style pipeline step "sample N docs uniformly, send to strong LLM" into "sample seed, loop: score pool with current classifier, select next batch by an active-search/uncertainty criterion, annotate, refit." It would also justify keeping the strong-model annotation budget the same while getting materially better rare-label filters (toxicity, contamination, licensed text) for the same dollar spend, which matters most exactly where labels are rarest and current single-pass random sampling is weakest.
