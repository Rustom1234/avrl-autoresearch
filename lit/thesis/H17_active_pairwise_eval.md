# H17: Active Pairwise Evaluation of Checkpoints and Model Variants

## 1. Hypothesis and SOFisher mapping

A policy that chooses which pair of candidate checkpoints to compare on which prompt next, exploiting that win rates are correlated across similar prompts and that model-quality rankings are approximately low rank, converges to a reliable ranking with far fewer human or judge comparisons than uniform (random or round-robin) matchmaking, and adapts online as new checkpoints arrive mid-training. This maps directly onto SOFisher: fields of view become (model pair, prompt) cells, spatial autocorrelation of rare targets becomes prompt-similarity autocorrelation of win rates, and the expensive fluorescence measurement becomes the expensive human or LLM-judge comparison, with cheap state features (embeddings, prompt metadata, proxy-judge scores) standing in for the cheap imaging features that cannot alone rank targets.

## 2. Prior art

- Chatbot Arena, Chiang et al., 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132 — Bradley-Terry ranking over crowdsourced pairwise battles; adaptive/uncertainty-driven sampling is used operationally to reduce comparisons needed to separate models, versus fully random matchmaking.
- Active ranking from pairwise comparisons, Heckel, Shah, Ramchandran, Wainwright (the "futility of parametric assumptions" line of work Szorenyi and others build on), Annals of Statistics 2019. https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-47/issue-6/Active-ranking-from-pairwise-comparisons-and-when-parametric-assumptions-do/10.1214/18-AOS1772.pdf — proves sample complexity O(sum_i Delta_i^-2 log(K/(delta*Delta_i))) for active top-ranking, and shows assuming a parametric (e.g. Bradley-Terry) model buys at most a logarithmic factor over no structural assumption at all.
- Preference-based Online Learning with Dueling Bandits: A Survey, Bengs, Busa-Fekete, El Mesaoudi-Paul, Hullermeier, JMLR 2021 (arXiv 1807.11398). https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.11398 — establishes the Omega(N log T) worst-case regret lower bound for dueling-bandit ranking under the Condorcet-winner assumption, the standard baseline class this thesis line would need to beat.
- Length-Controlled AlpacaEval, Dubois et al., 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475 — controlling for response length raises Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena human rankings from 0.94 to 0.98, i.e. a cheap feature (length) is a strong confound but not a sufficient ranking signal on its own.
- Arena-Hard-Auto, Li, Chiang, Frick, Dunlap, Zhu, Gonzalez, Stoica, LMSYS, 2024. https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-04-19-arena-hard/ — 500 curated prompts give 87.4% separability and 89.1% agreement with Chatbot Arena human rankings (94.1% Spearman correlation), versus 22.6% separability for MT-Bench, at roughly $25 per model evaluated; direct evidence that a small, well-chosen prompt subset preserves most of the ranking signal of a much larger comparison budget.
- Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena, Zheng, Chiang, Sheng et al., NeurIPS 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685 — GPT-4 judge agrees with human preference over 80% of the time, matching human-human agreement, but the paper documents position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases in that same judge.
- The Leaderboard Illusion, Singh, Nan, Wang, D'Souza, Kapoor, Ustun, Koyejo, Deng, Longpre, Smith, Ermis, Fadaee, Hooker, 2025. https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20879v1 — documents that Google and OpenAI received about 19.2% and 20.4% of all Arena battles respectively while 83 open-weight models combined received 29.7%, and that added private testing data produced relative performance gains up to 112% on the Arena distribution; this is direct evidence that current matchmaking in a live arena is already far from uniform and that the allocation policy materially changes measured rank.
- Large Language Model Selection with Limited Annotations (SELECT-LLM), Durmazkeser, Okanovic, Kirsch, Hoefler, Gurel, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24981v1 — an expected-information-gain policy computed from pairwise output similarities cuts annotation cost by up to 81.8% versus uniform sampling for model selection; this is the closest existing system to H17's proposed policy and should be treated as a strong baseline, not a gap to fill from scratch.
- A Unified Perturbation Framework for Analyzing Leaderboard Stability and Manipulation, Oyarhoseini, Lin, Karimi, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15761v1 — analyzes Bradley-Terry leaderboard stability under active-sampling perturbations, relevant to how an active policy could be gamed or destabilized by adversarial checkpoint submission.
- MINCE: Shrinking LLM Evaluation Datasets via Few-Model Monte Carlo Calibration, 2026 (exact arXiv id UNVERIFIED, found via search snippet only). Reported to achieve lower calibration drift than tinyBenchmarks-style item-response-theory subset selection; UNVERIFIED beyond that comparison claim since the full paper was not fetched directly.

## 3. What is genuinely open as of 2026

Static active-ranking theory (Heckel-style) and static active-selection systems (SELECT-LLM, Arena-Hard curation) both exist and work well. What is not yet established in the literature surfaced here is the online, non-stationary version SOFisher actually motivates: a policy that keeps a low-rank ranking estimate calibrated as brand-new checkpoints are injected mid-training (cold-start arms with no comparison history), while simultaneously deciding both which prompt cluster and which pair to query next, under a judge (not just human) cost model whose own bias (Zheng et al.'s position/verbosity/self-enhancement effects) can be correlated with the very autocorrelation structure the policy is trying to exploit. The Leaderboard Illusion paper shows non-uniform allocation already happens in deployed arenas and already distorts rank by over 100% relative performance in extreme cases; nobody has yet shown a principled active policy that is provably robust to that same distortion risk while still beating uniform sampling. Closing that gap, cold-start handling, judge-bias-aware autocorrelation modeling, and adversarial robustness together, is a multi-experiment, multi-quarter effort, which is why a year rather than a month is a reasonable ask.

## 4. Three-condition check

1. Targets (here, win-rate differences) are autocorrelated across similar prompts: supported indirectly by Arena-Hard's use of BERTopic clustering to select 500 prompts that still reach 89.1% agreement with the full Arena ranking, implying strong redundancy within prompt clusters, and directly by SELECT-LLM's use of pairwise output similarity as its information-gain signal (81.8% cost reduction achieved by exploiting exactly this redundancy).
2. The expensive measurement (human or judge comparison) costs far more than cheap state features: supported by Arena-Hard's own cost accounting (~$25 per model across 1,000 judged comparisons) against the near-zero cost of embedding- or metadata-based features, and by MINCE's explicit framing as a cost-reduction method relative to full-comparison evaluation (UNVERIFIED in detail).
3. Cheap features alone cannot rank targets: supported by Length-Controlled AlpacaEval, where length (a free feature) swings raw win rate from 22.9% to 64.3% depending on verbosity yet is explicitly not a valid ranking signal on its own, and by Zheng et al.'s documented judge biases showing even a cheap LLM-judge proxy diverges from true human preference in systematic, non-random ways.

## 5. Year plan

**Phase 1 (months 1-3, first experiment within a week on 4 A100s).** Build a simulator from public Chatbot Arena battle logs and Arena-Hard judgments (both already released with per-prompt, per-pair outcomes), replaying historical comparisons offline so an active policy can be evaluated without new human cost. Implement a low-rank Bradley-Terry ranking estimator plus an uncertainty- or information-gain-based pair/prompt selection policy (SELECT-LLM-style) as the candidate; the 4-A100 budget covers embedding computation for prompt similarity and fast re-fitting of the ranking model, not human annotation. Metric: number of simulated comparisons needed to reach a stable top-k ranking (defined as top-k membership and order unchanged for N consecutive additional comparisons, following the separability definition used by Arena-Hard). Strongest baseline: SELECT-LLM's expected-information-gain policy and, as a lower bar, Chatbot Arena's deployed adaptive sampling itself.

**Phase 2 (months 4-8).** Extend to the online, non-stationary setting: inject new checkpoints mid-simulation with no comparison history (cold start), test judge-bias-correlated autocorrelation (does the policy overfit to a biased judge's blind spots the way it would overfit to spatial autocorrelation of easy targets), and stress-test against the kind of allocation asymmetry documented in The Leaderboard Illusion.

**Phase 3 (months 9-12).** Validate on a live, small-scale human or mixed judge-plus-human loop inside one lab pipeline (not a public arena), comparing total comparisons to reach a stable ranking against that lab's current uniform or manual matchmaking practice, and write up robustness results against adversarial or self-interested checkpoint submission.

## 6. Kill criteria

Kill the thesis direction if, on the Phase 1 simulator, the active policy's comparisons-to-stable-top-k is not at least 30 to 50% lower than SELECT-LLM's already-published gains (i.e. it fails to beat a baseline that already exists), since that would mean the SOFisher-style dynamic/low-rank framing adds nothing over a known static active-selection method. Also kill if cold-start checkpoints (Phase 2) cannot be ranked competitively within a small fixed comparison budget, since online adaptation during training is the core motivating use case and a policy that only helps in the static-candidate-set case is just SELECT-LLM again.

## 7. Impact on a lab's stack

If it works, this replaces ad hoc or uniform arena-style matchmaking in a lab's internal checkpoint-comparison harness (the thing every RLHF or post-training team already runs) with a policy that cuts judge or human annotation spend for a fixed ranking confidence, and lets new checkpoints produced mid-training run be slotted into the live leaderboard with a small, targeted comparison budget instead of a full re-sweep. It would not replace Arena-Hard or Chatbot Arena themselves, it would sit upstream of them as the internal, cheaper triage step before a candidate is promoted to full external-style evaluation.
