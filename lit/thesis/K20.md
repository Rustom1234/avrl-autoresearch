# K20 — Adversarial Prior-Art Check: H20 Active Distillation

## Closest existing work

1. **Baykal, Trinh, Iliopoulos, Menghani, Vee (2022), "Robust Active Distillation."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01213
   Parameter-free, game-theoretic policy that queries teacher soft-labels for points "simultaneously informative and correctly labeled," with provable performance bounds. Abstract says it beats "state-of-the-art active learning and active distillation methods," implying an established subfield predating this paper. No exact number retrievable from the abstract (UNVERIFIED).

2. **Luccioli, Iyengar, Panley, Haberkorn, Ge, Crane, Sinha, Lee (2025), "LLM on a Budget: Active Knowledge Distillation for Efficient Classification of Large Text Corpora."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11574
   M-RARU: uncertainty sampling plus randomized accept/reject selects which inputs an LLM teacher labels next, tested against random sampling across five student model types. Achieves **up to 80% reduction in sample requirements** versus random sampling (verified from abstract).

3. **Liu, Kang, Zhao, Kuang, Jiang, Sun, Wu (2024), "EvoKD: Evolving Knowledge Distillation with Large Language Models and Active Learning."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06414
   Actively analyzes the student's current weaknesses each round, has the teacher synthesize new samples targeting them, with iterative feedback as training proceeds: a closed-loop, student-state-driven query policy that adapts round to round. No exact number retrievable from the abstract (UNVERIFIED); effectiveness reported only qualitatively (text classification, NER).

Runners-up, not fully checked this session: ELAD, explanation-guided active distillation on reasoning uncertainty, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13098 (2024, UNVERIFIED beyond abstract), and PGKD, an explicit "active learning routine between the student model and the LLM," https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05045 (2024, UNVERIFIED beyond abstract).

## Strongest kill reason

"Already done." Active, budget-constrained teacher-query selection using cheap uncertainty/disagreement signals, and, in EvoKD's case, re-targeting the policy to the student's current weaknesses each round, is not an open gap. It is a populated subfield running 2022 (Baykal, provable guarantees, benchmarked against prior "active distillation methods") through 2025 (M-RARU, 80% query reduction). H20 section 2, items 11-12, calls this area UNVERIFIED because "this session's web-search budget was exhausted." That gap does not survive a five-minute check: five on-point papers exist (2022, three from 2024, one from 2025).

## Judgment

H20 section 3's premise, "no closed-loop adaptive query-selection policy for distillation has been described," is false as stated. The narrower open question: no found paper tests this at frontier-teacher / billion-parameter open-weight-student scale with an embedding-neighborhood signal, or benchmarks against LESS/DSIR-style gradient selection as H20's Phase 1 proposes. Whether adaptive selection still beats LESS/DSIR at matched budget, at LLM instruction-tuning scale, is UNVERIFIED and is the narrowest surviving gap, but it is an incremental scaling/baseline study against a known mechanism, not a new one, since M-RARU and EvoKD already show adaptive/active selection beats random at smaller scale.
