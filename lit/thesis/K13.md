## Three closest works

1. Souly, Rando, Chapman, Davies et al., "Poisoning Attacks on LLMs Require a Near-Constant Number of Poison Samples," 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07192. Achieved: 250 poisoned documents reliably backdoor models from 600M to 13B parameters, 6B-260B training tokens, independent of dataset size. Confirmed via direct fetch: the paper explicitly proposes no defense, stating only "highlighting the need for more research on defences to mitigate this risk in future models." This is the paper that defines H13's threat model, and it self-certifies the gap as open.

2. "Delta-Influence: Unlearning Poisons via Influence Functions," 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13731. Achieved: validated across 3 vision-based poisoning attacks and 3 image datasets, benchmarked against 5 detection algorithms and 5 unlearning strategies, claims best unlearning in all settings (exact accuracy/AUC number UNVERIFIED, not visible at abstract level). Critically, this is a vision-classifier method, not LLM pretraining text, so it is not actually in-domain despite being the closest-named competitor.

3. "DQS: A Low-Budget Query Strategy for Enhancing Unsupervised Data-driven Anomaly Detection Approaches," 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05663. Achieved: a dissimilarity-based query strategy that outperforms baseline query strategies specifically in the small-budget regime (exact margin UNVERIFIED, not visible at abstract level). This is the closest true "budgeted/active auditing" analog found, but it targets general anomaly detection, not poisoning, and not LLM pretraining corpora.

## Strongest "already done / cannot work" argument

The realistic threat model H13 targets (arXiv:2510.07192's near-constant 250-document result) is built from individually fluent, non-templated documents that an attacker can disperse across independently-controlled sources at low marginal cost, per the split-view/frontrunning economics in Carlini et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10149. That is precisely the case where source/duplicate/template clustering, the entire premise a sequential policy needs to beat uniform sampling, is least likely to exist. Templated poison is already caught by standard dedup pipelines (Lee et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06499); the harder, realistic case may leave no cheap cluster signal at all, collapsing the sequential policy to uniform allocation. H13's own kill criterion 2 already names this exact failure mode, which is a bad sign: the authors of the source hypothesis have already identified the likeliest reason it fails, meaning a committee needs only ask "did you check clustering strength first" to stall the plan before Phase 1 ends.

## Verdict

No work found couples an adaptive/sequential decision policy to a poison-detection expensive test on LLM pretraining text specifically. Delta-Influence is vision-only; the defining threat-model paper (arXiv:2510.07192) explicitly declines to propose a defense; DQS is budget-aware but domain-general. The gap is real but unusually fragile: it depends entirely on an unmeasured empirical fact (real clustering strength at 250-doc scale) that the thesis's own kill criteria already flag as the load-bearing risk.
