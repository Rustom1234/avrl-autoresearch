# K03: Adversarial prior-art kill attempt on H03 (active/budgeted red-teaming)

## Closest work found (beyond what H03 already lists)

**1. ProbeLLM (2026), arXiv 2602.12966** ("Automating Principled Diagnosis of LLM Failures"). This is NOT in H03's prior-art list and is the most dangerous find. It uses hierarchical Monte Carlo Tree Search that explicitly balances "global exploration of new failure regions" against "local refinement of recurring error patterns" under a "limited probing budget," organizing discovered failures via "failure-aware embeddings and boundary-aware induction" into interpretable failure modes. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12966. This is structurally the exact sequential-decision-over-embedding-space-under-budget pattern that H03 Section 3 claims "no existing system" does. Domain tags are cs.CL/cs.SE, i.e. general LLM failure diagnosis (reasoning, coding, etc.), not specifically adversarial/safety red-teaming. UNVERIFIED: exact numeric results (failure-mode counts, % gain vs baseline) — PDF text extraction failed in the time budget; abstract makes only qualitative claims ("broader, cleaner, more fine-grained").

**2. Red-Bandit (Zhu et al. 2025/ACL 2026), arXiv 2510.07239.** Multi-armed bandit over attack-style LoRA experts, budget explicitly stated as up to 150 attempts per behavior with the bandit pretrained over 10,000 trials; reports SOTA ASR@10 on AdvBench/HarmBench and lower perplexity than prior methods. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07239. Already in H03's own list as "closest existing work" — confirmed independently here.

**3. Rainbow Teaming (Samvelyan et al. 2024), arXiv 2402.16822.** MAP-Elites quality-diversity search over a feature grid; attack success rate exceeding 90% across tested models, hundreds of diverse adversarial prompts, fine-tuning on the discovered set improves safety without hurting helpfulness. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16822. Already in H03's list; no judge-cost split, confirmed.

## Strongest "already done" reason

H03's central novelty claim (Sec. 3) is that nobody treats "where to spend the next expensive call across embedding space, given a budget and history so far" as a sequential decision problem the way SOFisher does field-of-view selection. ProbeLLM already does this — MCTS trading exploration of new failure regions against refinement of known ones, over failure-aware embeddings, under a probing budget. The claimed algorithmic gap is occupied, just not in the red-teaming/safety framing and (unverified) without a real cheap-proxy/expensive-judge cost asymmetry.

## Judgment: does a year-long thesis still have a defensible gap?

Partially. The mechanism H03 proposes to spend a year building (Phase 1-2: sequential/bandit policy over embedding space beating static-ranked/clustering baselines on distinct-failure-modes-per-call) already exists and reportedly works in ProbeLLM's adjacent domain, which weakens the novelty and the feasibility bet that this is genuinely unproven. What is not yet shown anywhere: (a) the same mechanism applied to adversarial safety failures specifically, with a real cheap-proxy-vs-expensive-human/judge cost asymmetry rather than uniform probing cost, and (b) closing the loop into measured downstream safety fine-tuning effect. That combination is the narrowest surviving gap, and it is narrower than H03 currently claims (H03 should re-scope Phase 1 as "port ProbeLLM's MCTS mechanism to the safety-judge-cost setting," not as inventing the sequential-policy idea from scratch).
