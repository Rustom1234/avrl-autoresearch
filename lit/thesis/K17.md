# K17: Kill Attempt on H17 (Active Pairwise Evaluation of Checkpoints)

## SELECT-LLM verification

Confirmed via arxiv.org/abs/2605.24981. Title "Large Language Model Selection with Limited Annotations," Durmazkeser, Okanovic, Kirsch, Hoefler, Gurel, submitted 24 May 2026. Abstract confirms an expected-information-gain query-selection policy from pairwise output similarities, with "annotation cost reductions up to 81.8% for best model selection and up to 84.78% for near-best model selection" across 23 datasets. UNVERIFIED whether that figure is measured against uniform sampling specifically or "the strongest baseline" generally; the fetched abstract text says the latter. Static candidate-set method: no claim of handling new candidates arriving mid-run.

## Two more close works found

1. "Investigating Non-Transitivity in LLM-as-a-Judge," Xu, Ruis, Rocktaschel, Kirk, arXiv 2502.14074 (submitted 19 Feb 2025, revised 5 Jun 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14074: Swiss-Wise Iterative Matchmaking (Swim), a dynamic pairwise matchmaking strategy for LLM-judge tournaments, raises Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena 95.0% to 96.4%, Kendall 82.1% to 86.3%, versus round-robin. Adaptive online arena matchmaking already deployed for LLM ranking; abstract does not describe cold-start handling.
2. "Sample-Efficient Alignment for LLMs," arXiv 2411.01493 (submitted 3 Nov 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01493: formulates LLM alignment/comparison as a contextual dueling-bandit problem with sample-efficient algorithms. Not framed as checkpoint-ranking evaluation, but the dueling-bandit-for-LLM-comparison precedent H17's search terms named.

ArXiv API queries for "non-stationary dueling bandit" + LLM, and "dueling bandit" + "model comparison," returned zero hits. Semantic Scholar API was rate-limited (HTTP 429) throughout the window and could not be queried, a real gap, not evidence of absence.

## Strongest reason a committee kills this

H17's own cited theory paper (Heckel, Shah, Ramchandran, Wainwright, Annals of Statistics 2019) proves a parametric/structural model (Bradley-Terry, and by extension low-rank) buys "at most a logarithmic factor" over structure-free active ranking. H17's Phase 1 kill criterion demands its low-rank policy beat SELECT-LLM, which already captures up to 81.8% of achievable cost reduction on 23 datasets, by a further 30-50%. Its own cited theory implies only a log-factor gain is realistic, not 30-50% beyond a baseline near that paper's reported ceiling. The kill bar may be unreachable by the mechanism proposed, using only literature the review itself assembled.

## Judgment on the online/non-stationary/cold-start gap

Defensible as the narrowest surviving claim. Neither SELECT-LLM, Swim, nor the dueling-bandit-alignment paper ranks a growing checkpoint set with cold-start arms injected mid-training under a shared low-rank estimate. But this is time-boxed absence of evidence (Semantic Scholar unreachable), not a swept literature.
