# K16: Adversarial prior-art hunt on H16 (active run monitoring)

## Verification of the two named papers

LLM-PRISM, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10390 ("LLM-PRISM: Characterizing Silent Data Corruption from Permanent GPU Faults in LLM Training," Tyagi, Hukerikar, Saxena, Huang, Shirvani, Tung, Zhu): confirmed real. It couples RTL-level GPU fault simulation with a stochastic injection engine in Megatron-LM across 7,664 training runs in FP16/BF16/FP8 to characterize how fault type, rate, and numeric format affect resilience. This is passive, retrospective fault characterization, not a sequential policy that chooses where to spend a diagnostic budget. It does not compete directly with H16's active-allocation claim.

"Training instability in deep learning follows low-dimensional dynamical principles," https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13160 (Zhang, Yao, Li, Yang, 2026): confirmed real. Abstract states "deviations in low-dimensional latent meta-states systematically precede observable performance collapse," across optimization, data, parametric, and learning-signal stability dimensions, via "controlled perturbation auditing." This is a characterization/theory paper, not an active monitoring system, but it is the more dangerous of the two for H16 (see fatal flaw below).

## One more close work

"Towards Robust LLM Post-Training: Automatic Failure Management for Reinforcement Fine-Tuning" (Zhang, Jia, Zhai, Fang, Zheng, Liu, Huang, Yu, Li, submitted May 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04431. Proposes RFT-FM, unifying "anomaly detection, failure diagnosis, and auto remediation in a closed loop" for RL fine-tuning, backed by a benchmark (RFT-FaultBench) showing "RFT failures are both observable from training dynamics and distinguishable through their empirical fault fingerprints." This is reactive failure management in the RFT phase, not pretraining, and not a budgeted sequential-allocation policy over (layer, step, shard). Closest in spirit to H16's "detect-and-act" framing but different training phase and no budget constraint.

No paper found in this pass trains a policy over which (layer, step, shard) to probe next under a fixed diagnostic budget, matching the review's own conclusion in section 3 of H16_active_run_monitoring.md.

## Strongest kill-adjacent reason

arXiv:2601.13160's core finding, that a small number of latent meta-states already predict collapse across optimization/data/parametric/learning-signal dimensions, threatens SOFisher's cost-asymmetry condition (H16 section 4), not the "someone already built the policy" condition. If a cheap, already-published low-dimensional projection is nearly sufficient to flag danger everywhere at once, a simple always-on threshold trigger on that signal (no RL, no per-layer expensive stats) is a candidate baseline that the thesis's listed baselines (fixed-interval monitoring, ZClip) do not include. UNVERIFIED whether this cheap signal is actually cheap enough to compute per-layer at every step in practice; the paper's own compute cost for the latent-state extraction was not confirmed in this pass.

## Judgment

The specific sequential/RL diagnostic-allocation idea has no direct prior art in what was checked here. The thesis survives, but the surviving gap is narrower than stated: Phase 1 kill criteria (H16 section 6) compare fixed-interval vs. oracle, but should also compare against a cheap latent-state-triggered spot-check baseline built from arXiv:2601.13160's method, since that is now the harder bar to beat.
