# H3: Budgeted Active Red-Teaming and Failure Discovery

## 1. Hypothesis and SOFisher mapping

Model failures (jailbreaks, harmful compliance, hallucination, reward-hacking behaviors) cluster in prompt-embedding space and by attack family, so a policy that chooses where to spend the next expensive evaluation can exploit that structure. A learned policy that allocates a fixed budget of expensive judge calls (strong judge, human rater, or long multi-turn probe) across prompt space, conditioned on what earlier probes found, will surface more distinct failure modes per judge call than random or static-ranked probing, and the discovered failures can feed safety training data selection.

SOFisher mapping: field-of-view choice on a tissue slide maps to prompt (or conversation-branch) choice in embedding space; the expensive fluorescent imaging step maps to the expensive judge/human/multi-turn call; the cheap state features (low-res scan) map to cheap proxies (perplexity, a small classifier, embedding distance to known failure clusters); spatial autocorrelation of rare targets maps to clustering of failures by attack family and semantic neighborhood.

## 2. Prior art

- Perez et al. 2022, Red Teaming Language Models with Language Models, EMNLP. RL-trained attacker LM generates test prompts; classifier-scored harm elicitation, no active/budgeted query allocation. https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.225/
- Hong et al. 2024 (Improbable AI), Curiosity-driven Red-teaming for Large Language Models, ICLR. Novelty-bonus RL raises test-case diversity over vanilla RL red-teaming; elicits toxic outputs from a heavily RLHF'd LLaMA2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19464
- Samvelyan et al. 2024, Rainbow Teaming, NeurIPS. Quality-diversity (MAP-Elites-style) search over a feature grid; reports attack success rate exceeding 90% across tested models and hundreds of diverse adversarial prompts; fine-tuning on the discovered set improves safety without hurting helpfulness. Black-box, no notion of an expensive-vs-cheap evaluation budget split. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16822
- Zou, Wang, Fredrikson, Kolter 2023, Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models (GCG). Gradient-based greedy coordinate search finds transferable adversarial suffixes; per the practitioner literature, GCG needs on the order of hours of optimization per suffix, i.e., search cost is real but is compute, not judge/human cost. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043 UNVERIFIED exact hour figure, sourced from secondary practitioner writeups not the paper itself.
- Lee et al. 2020 (JAIR) / Corso et al., Adaptive Stress Testing: Finding Likely Failure Events with Reinforcement Learning. RL formulated as an MDP over a simulator to find the most likely failure trajectory, far more sample-efficient than Monte Carlo; origin domain is autonomous vehicles/aircraft collision avoidance, not language models. https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02188
- Corso et al. 2019, Efficient Black-box Assessment of Autonomous Vehicle Safety, extends AST with importance-sampling-style efficiency gains for rare-event discovery. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.03618
- OpenAI, Diverse and Effective Red Teaming with Auto-generated Rewards and Multi-step RL. Auto-generated reward signal (diversity + effectiveness terms) replaces a single learned reward model to avoid reward hacking and mode collapse in red-team generation. https://cdn.openai.com/papers/diverse-and-effective-red-teaming.pdf
- Zhu et al. 2025 (ACL 2026), Red-Bandit: Test-Time Adaptation for LLM Red-Teaming via Bandit-Guided LoRA Experts. Multi-armed bandit selects among attack-style LoRA experts at test time; reports higher ASR@10 than prior SOTA on AdvBench/HarmBench under a stated budget (up to 150 attempts per behavior, bandit pretrained for 10,000 trials), plus lower perplexity (more human-readable) prompts. Closest existing work to "bandit over attack space under a budget," but the arm space is attack styles, not embedded prompt locations, and there is no cheap-feature/expensive-judge cost split. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07239
- Anonymous 2025, Active Testing of Large Language Models via Approximate Neyman Allocation. Optimal allocation of a fixed query budget across prompt-response pairs to minimize estimation error of aggregate metrics; framed as estimation, not failure-mode discovery/diversity. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.10075
- Li et al. 2024, Active Evaluation Acquisition for Efficient LLM Benchmarking. Actively selects which benchmark items to score next to predict full-benchmark performance with fewer judge calls; benchmarking efficiency framing, not red-teaming. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.05952
- Lee, J. et al. 2025, Instance-Optimal Estimation with Multiple LLM Judges on a Budget. Allocates a query budget across multiple LLM judges of varying cost/reliability per instance. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23362
- Anonymous 2026, LLM-as-Judge on a Budget. Cost-aware judging strategies under budget constraints; general evaluation, not framed for failure-mode discovery. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15481
- MultiBreak, 2026, a scalable multi-turn jailbreak benchmark: 10,389 multi-turn adversarial prompts spanning 2,665 distinct harmful intents, with an active-learning pipeline for expanding attack candidates, evidence that diversity/coverage of distinct intents is an explicit target metric in 2026 work. https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01687v1
- Zhang et al. 2025, Diversity Helps Jailbreak Large Language Models, NAACL. Shows diversity-promoting search increases attack success versus non-diverse search baselines, direct evidence for exploiting diversity/clustering structure. https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.238.pdf
- Jailbreaking Leaves a Trace, 2026. Internal hidden-state representations of jailbreak prompts separate from benign/refusal representations and form identifiable clusters usable for detection, direct evidence for the clustering premise in embedding space. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11495

## 3. What is genuinely open as of 2026

No existing system treats "where to spend the next expensive judge/human call across prompt-embedding space, given a fixed budget and everything found so far" as a sequential decision problem with a learned value function, the way SOFisher treats field-of-view selection. Rainbow Teaming and curiosity-driven red-teaming optimize diversity/novelty of what to generate next but do not separate a cheap always-available signal from an expensive rationed one, and they are not explicitly reward-per-judge-call maximizers. Red-Bandit is the closest: a bandit over attack styles, but its arms are discrete style categories set at design time, not a continuous or learned embedding-space policy, and it optimizes attack success rate, not distinct-failure-mode count under a hard judge-call cap. The active-testing/Neyman-allocation line optimizes statistical estimation of an aggregate metric, not discovery of qualitatively distinct failure types. Nobody has shown, on a controlled budget, that an RL/bandit policy over prompt-embedding regions beats a strong static-ranked or clustering-then-round-robin baseline on failure-modes-per-judge-call specifically, nor closed the loop into safety-training data selection with a measured downstream effect. That is a year of work because it requires: (a) building a cheap-feature-to-expensive-judge testbed with real cost asymmetry, (b) defining and validating a distinct-failure-mode metric that is not gameable, (c) training and ablating a sequential policy against several strong non-learned baselines, and (d) demonstrating the feedback loop into training data actually changes the resulting model's safety profile, each of which is its own multi-week iteration cycle.

## 4. Three-condition check

- Judge cost is real: Agent-as-a-Judge work reports human evaluation at $1,297.50 and 86.5 hours versus an LLM-agent judge at $30.58 and about 2 hours (2.29% of cost, 2.36% of time) for a comparable task, confirming a real, large, and heterogeneous cost gradient between cheap and expensive evaluators. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10934. "When Scanners Lie: Evaluator Instability in LLM Red-Teaming" separately shows LLM-judge scores on red-team outputs are noisy enough that naive single-call judging is unreliable, reinforcing why a rationed, carefully allocated expensive-judge budget (or human tie-breaks) matters. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14633
- Cheap features alone are weak: GCG-style optimized suffixes are non-fluent and can partly be caught by perplexity filters, but the broader jailbreak literature (role-play, encoding, persuasion, multi-turn drift families) shows attacks that evade simple lexical/perplexity cheap filters while still succeeding, and evaluator-instability results show even LLM-based cheap-ish judges disagree with stronger judges/humans, i.e., cheap proxies do not reliably rank true failures. This condition is PARTIALLY VERIFIED: I found strong qualitative evidence, not a controlled study isolating "cheap feature AUC on this exact task."
- Failures cluster: hidden-state and embedding-space analyses show jailbreak prompts form separable clusters from benign prompts and cluster among themselves by attack style, and multiple taxonomies (seven-family taxonomy, Search x Carrier axes) independently converge on discrete attack families, directly supporting spatial/semantic autocorrelation of failures. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11495

## 5. Year plan

Phase 1 (weeks 1-6, 4 A100s, under a week for the first experiment): build the testbed on one or two open models (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as targets), a cheap feature set (embedding of prompt, perplexity, a small trained classifier), and an expensive judge (a strong open judge model, e.g., a 70B-class model, standing in for a human rater) with an artificial per-call cost. First experiment: replicate Red-Bandit's bandit-over-styles baseline and a static-ranked baseline on a fixed judge-call budget (e.g., 500-2000 calls), measured only on distinct-failure-modes-per-judge-call (clustering discovered failures post hoc by embedding and counting novel clusters, not raw ASR). Phase 2 (months 2-6): train a sequential policy (contextual bandit or small DQN, mirroring SOFisher's architecture) over prompt-embedding regions that uses both the cheap features and the history of judge outcomes; ablate against Rainbow Teaming's quality-diversity search and curiosity-driven RL as additional baselines. Phase 3 (months 6-12): close the loop by feeding discovered failures into a safety fine-tuning/preference dataset and measure whether the resulting model has fewer residual distinct failure modes under a fresh red-team budget, plus test transfer to a second target model. Metric throughout: distinct failure modes discovered per expensive judge call, at fixed budget. Strongest existing baseline to beat: Red-Bandit's bandit-guided LoRA experts, since it is the only 2025-2026 method that is both budget-aware and adaptive to target-model responses.

## 6. Kill criteria

Kill if, after Phase 1, a simple static-ranked or clustering-then-round-robin baseline matches the learned policy's distinct-failure-modes-per-call within noise, meaning the sequential/adaptive component adds nothing. Kill if the cheap features turn out to have near-zero correlation with expensive-judge failure labels (condition 2 fails empirically), since then there is no signal for a policy to exploit. Kill if failures do not cluster in the chosen embedding space for the target models tested (condition 3 fails empirically) rather than by construction of the attack-generation method. Kill if the Phase 3 feedback loop produces no measurable change in the fine-tuned model's residual failure count versus a random-sample baseline of the same size.

## 7. Impact

If it works, a lab would replace flat or static-priority red-team queue allocation (e.g., round-robin across attack categories or human-picked priority lists) with a policy that reallocates its scarce strong-judge and human-rater budget in real time toward embedding regions most likely to contain undiscovered failure types, directly increasing the rate of distinct new failure modes found per dollar of red-team spend, and would use the policy's own discovered clusters as a principled, non-redundant slice of the next safety fine-tuning batch instead of hand-curated or purely adversarially-optimized examples.
