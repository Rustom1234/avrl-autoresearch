# K09: Adversarial Prior-Art Check on H9 (Budgeted Verification)

## What I verified

**VIP / Adaptive Rollout Allocation (ICLR 2026)**: title confirmed, "Adaptive Rollout Allocation for Online Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards" (Nguyen et al.). It allocates how many rollouts to sample per prompt to minimize gradient variance, not which sampled rollout gets an expensive judge versus a cheap proxy. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01601. Exact efficiency numbers UNVERIFIED, abstract withholds them.

**Trust or Escalate (ICLR 2025)**: confirmed static, offline cascade for one-shot evaluation, not embedded in an RL loop. On a Chatbot Arena subset it reaches about 80% coverage with over 80% human agreement using Mistral-7B as the cheap tier. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370. The $283K to $34K/month figure in H09's doc is from a separate survey (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13717), UNVERIFIED against that source in this pass.

**Heteroskedastic Signals in Budgeted LLM Verification (2026)**: confirmed evaluation-time selective-compute (which outputs get extra verification, tool calls, or human audit), tested on MBPP and MATH with Qwen3-8B, LLaMA3-8B, GPT-4o-mini as judges, not inside an RL loop, no GRPO/RLVR gradient updates. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15841. Key result: cost-stratified static thresholding beats naive uniform allocation by up to 17 points in strongly heterogeneous regions, while "global online adaptation yields inconsistent gains over static thresholding." A simple, non-learned, cost-aware rule already captures most of the win; learned/adaptive policies do not reliably beat it outside specific heterogeneity regimes.

## Strongest reason a committee kills this

H9's kill criterion only requires beating uniform-random allocation by 10%. 2606.15841 shows the real bar is a cheap, non-learned, cost-stratified threshold, and that online/adaptive methods, structurally what H9 proposes, do not reliably clear even that bar. A committee member who read this would say the criterion targets a strawman: a year of infrastructure to jointly train a policy with GRPO could be matched by an afternoon's static threshold. This is a genuine objection, not "already done", since nobody has run the static-threshold baseline inside an RL loop, so whether it transfers there is UNVERIFIED.

## Does the gap survive

VIP addresses rollout-count allocation, not judge-selection, a distinct problem, gap intact. Trust or Escalate is offline and static, not RL-loop-joint, gap intact. Neither paper measures RM-error autocorrelation across GRPO rollout groups; UNVERIFIED that anyone has. Surviving gap: jointly training a verification-budget policy inside a live GRPO/RLVR loop, benchmarked against a cost-stratified static threshold rather than uniform-random, while measuring whether RM-error autocorrelation across rollout clusters is strong enough to beat that baseline. That is a harder test than H9 pre-registers, and no paper found runs it.
