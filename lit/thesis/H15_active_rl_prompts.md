# H15: Active Prompt Selection for RL Post-Training

## 1. Hypothesis and SOFisher Mapping

A learned policy that chooses which prompts (or prompt-space regions) to roll out next in RLVR/GRPO, using cheap signals such as recent pass rate, embedding neighborhood, or a learned difficulty predictor, will improve the model faster per rollout than uniform sampling or static difficulty filtering, because learnability is spatially autocorrelated across similar prompts just as rare targets are spatially autocorrelated across tissue. The mapping to SOFisher: the DQN's "which FOV to image next" becomes "which prompt to roll out next," the expensive measurement (imaging a FOV) becomes the expensive measurement (many rollouts plus verifier/reward scoring for a prompt), and the cheap state feature (prior FOV content, tissue context) becomes the cheap state feature (pass-rate history, embedding neighborhood, difficulty estimate) that predicts value without paying the expensive cost.

## 2. Prior Art

1. **DeepSeek-R1** (Guo et al., arXiv:2501.12948) — introduces GRPO-based RLVR at scale; post-training cost only about 147K H800 GPU-hours, roughly an order of magnitude below contemporaries. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948
2. **DAPO** (Yu et al., arXiv:2503.14476) — dynamic sampling drops zero-variance (all-correct or all-wrong) prompts and refills the batch; reaches 50 points on AIME 2024 with Qwen2.5-32B using 50% of the training steps DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B needed (47 points). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14476
3. **Prompt Curriculum Learning (PCL)** (Gao et al., arXiv:2510.01135) — a learned value model picks prompts near 50% success rate without rollouts; 12.1x and 16.9x faster than rollout-based filtering on MATH and DeepScaleR respectively. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01135
4. **AdaRFT** (Shi et al., arXiv:2504.05520) — adaptive curriculum reinforcement finetuning that adjusts problem difficulty from recent reward signal; reports improved training efficiency and final accuracy on AMC/AIME/IMO-style math versus fixed-order RFT. UNVERIFIED exact magnitude (abstract does not give a single headline percentage). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05520
5. **Self-Evolving Curriculum (SEC)** (arXiv:2505.14970) — learns a curriculum policy concurrently with RL fine-tuning across three reasoning domains (planning, induction, math); reports better generalization to harder OOD problems and better multi-domain skill balance than fixed curricula. UNVERIFIED specific percentage (abstract omits a single headline number). https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14970
6. **MoPPS** (arXiv:2507.04632) — Bayesian risk-predictive framework, models each prompt's success rate as a latent variable and does streaming posterior sampling in a multi-armed bandit to pick prompts without extra LLM calls; reports accelerated training with reduced rollouts across math, planning, and vision-geometry tasks. UNVERIFIED exact speedup number (not given in abstract). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04632
7. **Small Generalizable Prompt Predictive Models (PPM)** (arXiv:2602.01970) — a small model trained to predict prompt difficulty that generalizes across prompts and adapts as the policy evolves, used to steer efficient RL post-training of large reasoning models. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01970
8. **No Prompt Left Behind / RL-ZVP** (arXiv:2509.21880, ICLR 2026) — rather than discarding zero-variance prompts, extracts signal from them via entropy-guided advantage shaping; up to 8.61 points accuracy and 7.77 points pass-rate improvement over plain GRPO. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21880
9. **SPEED-RL** (arXiv:2506.09016) — online curriculum learning for reasoning-model RL; 2x to 6x faster training without degrading accuracy. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09016
10. **Reinforce-Ada** (arXiv:2510.04996) — adaptive sampling framework for REINFORCE-style LLM training under non-linear objectives, allocating rollout budget per prompt instead of uniformly. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04996
11. **Beyond Solvability: Task Learnability as a Static Prior** (arXiv:2608.09217) — argues learnability (not raw solvability/difficulty) is the right static prior for prompt selection in LLM RL post-training. https://pith.science/paper/2608.09217
12. **Teacher-Student Curriculum Learning** (Matiisen, Oliver, Cohen, Schulman, arXiv:1707.00183, 2017) — treats an N-task curriculum as an N-armed bandit and a teacher agent selects tasks by tracking learning-progress signals; predates LLM RLVR but is the direct ancestor of "policy picks what to train on next." https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.00183
13. **Automated Curriculum Learning for Neural Networks** (Graves, Bellemare, Menick, Munos, Kavukcuoglu, ICML 2017) — formalizes automatic curricula via the EXP3.S adversarial-bandit algorithm over loss-driven and complexity-driven progress signals for supervised sequence tasks. UNVERIFIED exact arXiv id (commonly cited as arXiv:1704.03003; not independently re-confirmed in this session).
14. **Prioritized Experience Replay** (Schaul, Quan, Antonoglou, Silver, arXiv:1511.05952, ICLR 2016) — prioritizes replay of high-TD-error transitions in DQN; outperforms uniform replay on 41 of 49 Atari games, the canonical precedent for "not all training examples are worth equal sampling weight." https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05952
15. **SOFisher** (Ng et al., Nature Communications 2026, s41467-026-73404-6) — the source analogy: a DQN picks the next field of view to image on a tissue slide under a fixed imaging budget, exploiting spatial autocorrelation of rare targets; on a real Alzheimer's dataset it guided FOV selection toward neurofibrillary tangles and amyloid-beta plaques more efficiently than dense-sampling baselines. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73404-6

## 3. What Is Genuinely Open as of 2026

The 2025-2026 literature already establishes, repeatedly, that (a) filtering to intermediate-difficulty or nonzero-variance prompts helps (DAPO, RL-ZVP), and (b) a learned online difficulty/learnability predictor beats static filtering and uniform sampling in isolated single-paper setups (PCL, AdaRFT, MoPPS, PPM, SEC, SPEED-RL). What is not yet settled: none of these papers frame prompt selection explicitly as a sequential decision problem that exploits autocorrelation in prompt-embedding space the way SOFisher exploits spatial autocorrelation on a slide — most use per-prompt scalar difficulty trackers or bandits over discrete task buckets, not a policy that generalizes "this prompt was hard, so are its embedding neighbors" to *unseen* prompts before ever rolling them out. There is no published head-to-head across adaptive-learnability, static-difficulty-filter, and uniform-sampling baselines on a common model/dataset/compute budget; each paper compares its own method against its own weaker baselines, so relative gains are not cleanly comparable. It is also unverified whether these gains persist at the compute scales frontier labs actually run (most experiments in items 3-9 above are single-digit-billion-parameter models on math-only prompt sets), and whether an embedding-neighborhood generalization signal (the closest analogue to SOFisher's spatial autocorrelation) adds anything over a per-prompt scalar tracker once bandit-style updating is already in place. A year is needed to: build the common benchmark harness, test whether embedding-neighborhood transfer actually beats scalar-bandit curricula (the genuinely novel SOFisher-flavored claim), and test whether gains hold as the policy scales past 1.5-7B and past math-only domains.

## 4. Three-Condition Check

- **Targets spatially/structurally autocorrelated**: Evidence is indirect but consistent — PCL's value model transfers difficulty estimates across prompts without per-prompt rollouts (arXiv:2510.01135), and MoPPS's latent success-rate model generalizes across math, planning, and vision-geometry tasks (arXiv:2507.04632), both implying nearby prompts (in embedding or skill space) share learnability. No paper found explicitly measures an embedding-distance-vs-learnability-correlation curve; this specific claim is UNVERIFIED and is exactly the gap in section 3.
- **Expensive measurement dominates cost versus cheap state features**: Confirmed structurally by RLVR's own mechanics — DAPO explicitly wastes full rollout batches on zero-variance prompts before it can even observe them are uninformative (arXiv:2503.14476), and GRPO requires multiple rollouts plus reward scoring per prompt versus a nearly-free pass-rate history lookup, matching SOFisher's cost asymmetry (expensive FOV imaging vs cheap prior-FOV features).
- **Cheap features alone cannot rank targets, a policy is needed**: Supported by the fact that all named methods (PCL, AdaRFT, MoPPS, PPM, SEC) build a *learned, updating* predictor rather than a one-shot static filter, and RL-ZVP shows a naive cheap heuristic (drop zero-variance prompts) actually loses signal versus a smarter policy (arXiv:2509.21880, up to 8.61 points gain from not naively filtering). This condition is the best-supported of the three.

## 5. Year Plan

**Phase 1 (weeks 1-6, first experiment on 4 A100s, under one week):** GRPO on Qwen2.5-1.5B (or similar open 1.5B base) on a math prompt pool (MATH + a GSM8K-derived expansion for volume), three conditions: (a) uniform prompt sampling, (b) static difficulty filter (drop prompts outside a fixed pass-rate band, DAPO-style), (c) adaptive learnability policy (bandit or lightweight value model updating pass-rate estimates per prompt plus embedding-neighborhood smoothing, PCL/MoPPS-style). Primary metric: eval accuracy on held-out MATH/AIME-style problems per rollout-budget spent (sample efficiency curve), not per wall-clock step, to isolate the policy's effect from engineering speed. Strongest existing baseline to beat: DAPO's dynamic sampling (arXiv:2503.14476) and PCL (arXiv:2510.01135), since both are open, reproducible, and already report large speedups against uniform/static baselines.

**Phase 2 (months 2-6):** if Phase 1 shows adaptive beats static-filter beats uniform with a clear rollout-efficiency gap, scale to 7B and add a second domain (code, via a verifiable unit-test reward) to test whether the learned policy's embedding-neighborhood signal transfers across domains or must be relearned. Ablate the SOFisher-specific claim directly: adaptive-with-embedding-neighborhood vs adaptive-scalar-only (no neighborhood transfer), to isolate whether spatial-autocorrelation exploitation adds anything over a plain per-prompt bandit.

**Phase 3 (months 6-12):** if the neighborhood signal earns its keep, integrate into a production-scale RLVR run (13B+, mixed math/code/reasoning prompts) alongside DAPO-style dynamic sampling as a combined system, and write up a comparison against MoPPS/PPM/PCL under one shared harness, the comparison the current literature lacks (section 3).

## 6. Kill Criteria

Kill after Phase 1 if the adaptive policy's rollout-efficiency curve is statistically indistinguishable from DAPO-style static dynamic filtering (i.e., the marginal value of a *learned, updating* policy over a fixed all-correct/all-wrong drop rule is near zero) — this would mean SOFisher's "policy beats heuristic" story does not transfer, since RLVR's heuristic (drop zero-variance) is already cheap and already strong. Also kill if the embedding-neighborhood component in Phase 2 adds no measurable gain over a scalar per-prompt bandit, since that specific mechanism is the one part of H15 not already well covered by 2025-2026 prior art.

## 7. Impact on a Lab's Stack

If it works, this slots directly into any existing GRPO/RLVR training loop as a prompt-sampler component upstream of the rollout worker, no change to the policy-gradient math itself (unlike novel RL algorithms, it is a data-scheduling layer). A validated version would let a small lab cut rollout/scoring compute (the single most expensive line item in RLVR post-training) by an amount plausibly in the same range as the 2x-12x figures already reported piecemeal in DAPO, PCL, and SPEED-RL, which matters most for labs without frontier-scale rollout budgets. It composes with, rather than replaces, existing DAPO-style dynamic filtering and reward-shaping tricks (RL-ZVP), so the deliverable is an additive module, not a rip-and-replace.
