# K10: Prior-Art Kill Attempt on H10 (Sequential Annotation)

## Three closest works

**1. SIEVE, "GPT-4o as the Gold Standard" (Zhang et al., Oct 2024).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02755
Runs exactly the loop H10 proposes: fine-tune a lightweight classifier on labels collected so far, select the next documents near a class-imbalance-aware decision threshold ("True Risk Minimizer" sampling), query GPT-4o, repeat until budget exhausted (Algorithm 1, "for r=1,...,T/B do"). Applied directly to DCLM-style pretraining filtering, on tasks with minority-class rates as low as roughly 2.6-4.3% (Climate, AI filters). UNVERIFIED specific numbers from the paper's own tables, need primary-source confirmation: about 500 filtering operations per one GPT-4o-equivalent call; DCLM Core benchmark score improves from 0.1761 (DCLM-baseline) to 0.1790 with SIEVE, beating FineWeb-Edu's 0.1685. If these numbers hold, SIEVE already demonstrates classifier-level AND downstream-benchmark-level gains from a closed active-annotation loop over the FineWeb-Edu/DCLM-style random-sample baseline, which is most of H10's three-phase claim compressed into one paper.

**2. "LLM on a Budget" / M-RARU (2025).** https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11574v1
Already cited in H10's own prior-art list. Active learning (uncertainty plus accept/reject sampling) combined with LLM-teacher distillation for a student text classifier; UNVERIFIED report of up to 80% reduction in labeled-sample requirements vs random sampling on two classification benchmarks (Public Comments, GNAD). Same refit-select-query-repeat template as H10, but generic classification rather than pretraining-corpus filtering.

**3. GALAXY / classical active search (Garnett 2012, Jiang 2017, Ding et al. 2022).** https://icml.cc/2012/papers/618.pdf, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01402
Already cited by H10 itself. Decision-theoretic and graph-based rare-class discovery is a 14-20 year old subfield; GALAXY explicitly targets extreme class imbalance at graph scale, well outside text-corpus filtering but the same query-strategy math H10 leans on for Phase 2.

## Strongest kill argument

SIEVE already runs the closed loop, already applies it to a DCLM/FineWeb-adjacent pretraining pipeline, already handles minority rates approaching the thesis's target rarity band, and already reports a downstream benchmark-score improvement over the exact random-sample baseline H10 names as "the strongest deployed baseline." Section 3 of H10 states flatly "No published pipeline runs a closed active-annotation loop... for corpus-scale pretraining filters." SIEVE (Oct 2024, predates H10) appears to falsify that sentence directly. This is not the generic "active learning is 20 years old" objection; it is a "your Phase 1-2 core deliverable was already built and benchmarked by someone else" objection.

## Judgment

A defensible gap survives, but it is narrower than claimed. SIEVE's tested minority rates (roughly 2.6-45.7%) do not reach the thesis's target rarity (contamination/severe toxicity under 1%), its tasks are topic/quality filters (Climate, AI, Politics, Quality) not safety/legal ones, and it is UNVERIFIED whether SIEVE compares against an annotation-dollar-matched random baseline rather than a fixed-cost-reduction framing. The narrow surviving gap: closed-loop annotation at true sub-1% base rates, on safety/legal label types, against an explicit budget-matched (not just cost-reduced) random baseline, with safety-specific downstream evals. Phase 1 and 2 of the year plan need a rewrite that positions the work as a SIEVE extension, not a first demonstration, before this is fundable as stated.
